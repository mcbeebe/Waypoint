## 0. Read this first — three corrections to the plan of record

The grant stack named in `Payer-Funded-Pivot-Review-Aug2026.md` §5.7 was written in August 2026
without verification. Three of its four named sources are wrong or unusable as stated:

| Claim in §5.7 | Verified reality (Sept 7, 2026) |
|---|---|
| "CalOSBA" as a funding source | **Wrong.** CalOSBA's two grant programs — the Technical Assistance Program (TAP) and Capital Infusion Program (CIP) — are *not grants to small businesses*. TAP funds designated technical-assistance centers (federally designated, or 501(c)(3)/(c)(6)); CIP funds only SBDC Lead Centers. Both require 1:1 match. The one CalOSBA program that did pay small businesses directly, the **California Dream Fund** ($10k microgrants), concluded its original $35M round in April 2026. |
| "WITH Foundation" | **Poor fit, twice over.** WITH funds (a) **501(c)(3) nonprofits only** and (b) healthcare for **adults** with I/DD. Waypoint is a for-profit serving families of *children*. Not reachable without both a nonprofit arm and a scope change. |
| "foundation grants (Lucile Packard, local family foundations)" | **Directionally right, structurally blocked.** LPFCH's CYSHCN program is close to a perfect mission match, but private foundations overwhelmingly require 501(c)(3) status, and fiscal sponsorship is generally *not* available to LLCs and for-profit entities (policies vary by funder; California Arts Council, for example, excludes LLCs and for-profits from sponsored applications outright). |
| "NIH STTR (14.8% success, Jan 2027 deadline)" | **Deadline right, everything else stale.** SBIR/STTR **lapsed entirely on Sept 30, 2025**; NIH terminated all open SBIR/STTR NOFOs effective Oct 1, 2025 (NOT-OD-26-006). Congress reauthorized via S.3971, signed **April 13, 2026**, through **Sept 30, 2031**. NIH then reissued the parent NOFOs under **new numbers** (`PA-27-100` SBIR, `PA-27-102` STTR). Any application drafted against the old numbers is void. |

**The one source §5.7 got right, and undersold:** the **DDS Service Access & Equity (SAE) grant
program** explicitly defines an eligible community-based organization as *"a public or private
nonprofit, **or private for-profit** organization."* It is the single best-fit money in this
document and it is open to Waypoint as a for-profit, with no nonprofit arm required.

## 1. The structural fact that shapes everything below

Waypoint is a **for-profit** company. That is not a neutral choice for grant purposes — it splits
the funding universe cleanly in two:

- **Open to Waypoint as-is:** federal SBIR/STTR (statutorily *restricted* to for-profit small
  business concerns — a nonprofit is ineligible), DDS SAE (for-profits explicitly named), CHCF
  Innovation Fund (invests in companies), and commercial small-business grants.
- **Closed to Waypoint as-is:** essentially every private foundation — LPFCH, WITH, Blue Shield of
  California Foundation, most family foundations. These need 501(c)(3), and a for-profit generally
  cannot borrow one through fiscal sponsorship.

**Implication:** do not budget staff time chasing foundation money in FY27. The realistic FY27
pipeline is **federal SBIR + DDS SAE + CHCF + in-kind credits**. Foundation money becomes reachable
only if and when Waypoint stands up a separate 501(c)(3) affiliate — a real option (the Khan
Academy / Mozilla-style hybrid), but a second entity with its own board, its own filings, and
arm's-length transfer-pricing discipline between the two. That is a FY28 decision, not a FY27 one.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Funder** | NIH / CDC / FDA omnibus. Relevant institutes: **NICHD** (child health, IDD) and **NIMH**. |

### 1.2 NIH Parent STTR — `PA-27-102` (R41/R42)

Same cycle and caps as above. Differs in two ways that matter:

- **Requires a research-institution partner**, with ≥30% of work at the institution and ≥40% at the
  small business.
- **The PI may sit at the university** (with ≥10% effort) — verified in
  `Roadmap/Assumptions-Audit-Aug2026.md` (M8) and confirmed here.

**When to prefer STTR over SBIR:** if a UC/CSU partner (a disability-studies, health-services, or
special-education research group) co-authors the outcomes design. That partner also solves the
credibility problem a solo-founder application has. **When to prefer SBIR:** if no partner is
lined up by November 2026 — do not manufacture a partnership to fit the mechanism.

## 4. Tier 3 & 4 — small, fast, and in-kind

### Tier 3 — commercial small-business grants (LLC-eligible, no 501(c)(3))

- **[Hello Alice](https://www.helloalice.com/small-business-grants-and-funding)** — rolling grant

- **Amber Grant** — three $10,000 grants monthly, closing 11:59pm ET on the last day of each month.
  **Conditional:** requires a woman aged 18+ owning **≥50%** of the business. Applies to Waypoint

  application site as of Aug 31, 2026.

## 5. The critical path — registrations gate everything federal

None of the federal money in §2 is reachable without a legal entity, an EIN, and a registration
chain that must be done **in order**, because each system depends on the one before it:

1. **Legal entity + EIN** — prerequisite for everything below.
2. **SAM.gov** (issues the **UEI**) — **3 weeks typical, 6+ weeks worst case.**
3. **eRA Commons** (NIH) — **2+ weeks.** NIH treats starting ≥10 business days before a due date as
   a good-faith effort.
4. **SBA Company Registry** — 1–2 business days; issues the SBC Control ID required on the
   application.
5. **Grants.gov** — after SAM.

**Total: 4–6+ weeks, assuming nothing goes wrong.** Source:
[NIH organization registrations](https://grants.nih.gov/new-to-nih/organization-registration) ·
[required company registrations](https://seed.nih.gov/small-business-funding/how-to-apply/before-you-apply/register-company).

### Working backward from January 5, 2027

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **By ~Oct 15, 2026** | Entity formed, EIN issued. *(This is the binding constraint on the entity decision.)* |
| Oct 15 – Nov 30, 2026 | SAM.gov → eRA Commons → SBA Registry → Grants.gov |
| Oct – Dec 2026 | Draft the SBIR/STTR aims; decide SBIR vs STTR by ~Nov 15 based on whether a research partner is real |
| **Jan 5, 2027** | NIH SBIR/STTR submission |

## 6. Honest expected value

Grants are **bridge funding, not a channel** — `Options-Stack-Rank-Aug2026.md` ranks them #10 of 10
and says *"Pursue in parallel; never plan on it."* That ranking stands, with one amendment: the
**DDS SAE program** is better-fit and more winnable than that row implies, because for-profits are
eligible, the disparity data makes the case for Waypoint rather than against it, and $11M/year is
distributed across many modest awards rather than a few large ones.

Realistic FY27 read:

- **Highest probability:** in-kind credits (near-certain, days), Hello Alice (low value, low effort).
- **Best expected value:** DDS SAE — but the next window is ~a year out, so the work now is
  relationship-building, not writing.
- **Biggest prize, longest odds:** NIH SBIR/STTR. Historic NICHD STTR success rates in the ~15% band
  (unverified — check [NIH RePORTER](https://reporter.nih.gov)). One application per cycle is the
  right cadence; two is a distraction from revenue.
- **Most strategically valuable per dollar:** CHCF — the network into Medi-Cal managed care is worth
  more than the capital, and it is the only Tier-1 source with no deadline.

**Do not** let grant writing displace the W1b gate items (first family through the pipeline, first
paid invoice). A paid invoice is worth more than a submitted application.
