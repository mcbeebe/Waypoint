## 2. Scope of review (restated in full — this is the main delta)

Every Spanish item is the translation twin of an English page that has
already passed credentialed review (the site pairs them via a shared
translation key; the ES review references the approved EN version's git
SHA). The ES reviewer verifies, for each assigned item:

**a. Substantive accuracy carried over.** The Spanish text makes the same
legal/benefits claims as the approved English version — no meaning drifted
in translation. Statute citations (WIC §4643-style), deadlines, dollar
figures, and eligibility rules match the approved EN source exactly.

**b. Translation fidelity.** The Spanish is a faithful rendering of the
reviewed English meaning — not a looser paraphrase, not a stricter one.
Where a literal rendering would mislead, the reviewer flags it and proposes
phrasing that preserves the legal meaning.

**c. Terminology correctness and consistency.** Program and legal terms are
rendered the way California agencies and Spanish-speaking families actually
encounter them (e.g., Regional Center / Centro Regional; IEP kept as "IEP"
with a plain-language gloss; Medi-Cal never "translated"). The reviewer
helps maintain and is bound by the shared ES terminology glossary
[TBC — glossary location; create at onboarding if it does not exist].

**d. Cultural and register appropriateness for California Spanish-speaking
families.** Plain-language register (matching the site's grade 7–8 target),
broadly neutral Spanish rather than region-specific idioms, `usted`/`tú`
consistency per the site style choice [TBC — style decision], and examples
or tone that respect how these families actually engage with schools,
Regional Centers, and benefits offices. Fear-mongering or condescension
introduced by translation is a reject.

**e. `[TBC]` markers** within the reviewer's expertise: confirm, correct,
or strike — same rule as the EN role.

**Out of scope:** same exclusions as the base document §2 (no copyediting
beyond the register duties above, no authorship, no advice to readers, no
review of the app). Additionally out of scope: re-reviewing the substantive
legal accuracy of the English source — that was the EN reviewer's sign-off;
the ES reviewer flags suspected EN errors rather than re-adjudicating them
(flags are compensated findings, routed back to the EN review queue).
