<h2 class="qa-h" id="what-is-medi-cal">What is Medi-Cal?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  <b>Medi-Cal is California's version of Medicaid</b> — the health insurance program for children
  with developmental disabilities. It covers therapy, equipment, medical care, and specialized
  services. The complication: Medi-Cal has income limits, and many middle-income families get turned
  away because their earnings are "too high." But there's a solution: <b>institutional deeming</b>, a
  rule that lets your child qualify anyway, without counting your income at all <Cite>DHCS Medi-Cal
  Eligibility</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

To qualify, your child must either have income below the threshold (standard path) OR be a Regional
Center client (institutional deeming path). Either way, getting Medi-Cal is the gateway to benefits
that change everything: IHSS (which can pay you as a caregiver), therapy, and services private
insurance won't cover.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="what-it-unlocks">What does Medi-Cal unlock?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Medi-Cal is the eligibility key for <b>IHSS</b> — including
  <a href="/guides/benefits/ihss-protective-supervision/">protective supervision</a>, which can
  pay a parent for supervision hours — plus Medi-Cal-covered therapies, equipment, and
  services that private insurance denies or caps. This is why deeming is worth pursuing even
  if your child is already insured.
</AnswerFirst>

Many families use Medi-Cal as **secondary** coverage: present both insurance cards to providers,
let private insurance pay first, then Medi-Cal covers remaining copayments, coinsurance, and
deductibles. Having private insurance does not increase Medi-Cal cost or affect eligibility —
Medi-Cal remains free. Institutional deeming status works the same way as primary coverage
<Cite>Undivided Medi-Cal Secondary Coverage Guide</Cite>. And to retire the most stubborn myth in
this system one more time: your child does **not** need SSI to get IHSS — Medi-Cal is the
requirement, and deeming is a route to Medi-Cal that ignores your income entirely. The short
version lives at
<a href="/answers/does-my-child-need-ssi-to-get-ihss/">Does my child need SSI to get IHSS?</a>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="who-qualifies">Does my child qualify?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Medi-Cal has two entry points for children with developmental disabilities: <b>standard
  eligibility</b> (if your family income is below the limit) and <b>institutional deeming</b> (if
  you're over the limit but your child is a Regional Center client) <Cite>DHCS Medi-Cal
  Eligibility, DHCS HCBS-DD Waiver</Cite>. Most families fit one of these paths.
</AnswerFirst>

**Path 1: Standard eligibility (income-based)**
Your child qualifies if your household income is at or below 138% of the federal poverty level
(about $36,777 annually for a family of 3 in 2026). This path is fast: apply directly to your
county or Covered California with no Regional Center involvement needed.

**Path 2: Institutional deeming (waiver-based)**
Your child qualifies if:
- They are a **Regional Center client under 18** living at home with family
- Your family income is **above the Medi-Cal threshold** (the standard limit)
- They receive **at least one funded Regional Center service per year**
- The child's **own income** is minimal (usually $0)

If your child isn't yet a Regional Center consumer, start there first: our
<a href="/guides/regional-centers/">Regional Center guide</a> walks the eligibility rules and
the decision clock, and the intake call is free regardless of income.

**Early Start children:** Children age 0–3 in Early Start can qualify for institutional deeming if
the Regional Center evaluates them under Lanterman Act criteria. Ask your service coordinator for a
"Status 2" determination, which opens the deeming pathway before age 3 <Cite>DHCS Eligibility
Procedures Manual</Cite>.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="what-is-it">What is institutional deeming?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Institutional deeming is a rule that <b>turns off the income test for Regional Center clients</b>.
  Normally, Medi-Cal counts parents' income when a child applies. Under an HCBS waiver for people
  with developmental disabilities, your child is treated as if they lived in a medical facility —
  <b>only the child's own income and resources count</b>, while they stay at home with you
  <Cite>DHCS HCBS-DD Waiver</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

The name is bureaucratic, so here is the plain version. Decades ago, a child in an institution
qualified for Medicaid on their own income — which for most children is zero — while the same
child at home was blocked by their parents' paychecks. Waivers were created to fix that backwards
incentive: they let the "institutional" counting rules follow the child home. That's the whole
trick, and it's why the rule feels too good to be true when a parent first hears about it. It
isn't. It's the system correcting itself.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="do-you-need-deeming">Do you actually need institutional deeming?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Before you pursue the waiver, check one thing: <b>is your family income above or below the
  Medi-Cal threshold?</b> If you're below, your child qualifies for regular Medi-Cal — no waiver,
  no Regional Center paperwork. Only families above the threshold need institutional deeming.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="pathway-below-threshold">If your income is below the threshold: Apply online</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Your child qualifies for standard Medi-Cal. Skip the Regional Center waiver — apply directly
  through <b>Covered California</b> (the state's unified portal) or your county social services.
</AnswerFirst>

1. **Start at <a href="https://www.coveredca.com">Covered California</a> (the easiest route).**
   This is the state's single application for Medi-Cal. You can apply year-round, online or by phone:
   **<a href="tel:1-800-300-1506">1-800-300-1506</a>**. The application takes about 20 minutes.
2. **Answer questions about household income and size.** Include both parents' income if applicable.
   The system will determine if your child qualifies for Medi-Cal and enroll them automatically.
3. **Wait for your approval notice.** The state typically responds within 30–45 days. Watch your mail
   for any requests for additional information — response windows are short (usually 10 days).
4. **Save your award letter.** Once approved, your Medi-Cal award letter is your key to IHSS and
   other services <Cite>DHCS Medi-Cal Eligibility</Cite>.

**Backup option:** If you prefer to work with your county directly, use the
<a href="https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/medi-cal/contact/find-a-county-office/">DHCS County Office Finder</a>
to locate your local social services department (phone, mail, or in-person application available).

<h2 class="qa-h" id="pathway-above-threshold">If your income is above the threshold: Pursue institutional deeming</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Your family income is too high for regular Medi-Cal, but institutional deeming opens a path.
  Your Regional Center service coordinator is the starting point — they handle the waiver enrollment.
</AnswerFirst>

1. **Ask your service coordinator, in writing, to start institutional deeming.** Using the
   exact term routes the request correctly, and a written ask creates the paper trail this
   system responds to. Example: <i>"I'd like to pursue Medi-Cal through institutional deeming for my child under the HCBS-DD waiver."</i>
2. **Your RC coordinator enrolls your child in the waiver.** This step takes a few weeks. Your
   coordinator will confirm once the enrollment is active.
3. **File the child's Medi-Cal application.** Under deeming the child applies on their own
   income — answer income questions for the child, not the household. Your Regional Center sends
   form DHCS 7096 (the waiver referral) to the county; make sure to reference it or upload a copy
   when you apply through <a href="https://www.benefits.ca.gov">BenefitsCal</a> so the county
   applies deeming rules <Cite>DHCS Institutional Status Procedures</Cite>.
4. **Watch for the county counting your income anyway.** It's the most common failure mode:
   an eligibility worker processes the application as a regular child case. If a denial cites
   parental income, don't accept it — reply that this is an institutional-deeming case through
   the Regional Center waiver, and loop your service coordinator in.
5. **Keep the award letter with your records.** Medi-Cal in hand is what unlocks the next
   doors — see below.

<EmpathyCallout label="From a parent who's been there">
  "An eligibility worker told me flatly that we earned too much. She wasn't lying — she just
  didn't know this rule, because almost nobody does. The parents who get it are the parents
  who name it. You're allowed to know a rule the person across the counter doesn't."
</EmpathyCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-denied">What if the county says no?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Get the denial <b>in writing</b>, check whether it wrongly counted parental income, and file a
  <b>State Fair Hearing request with the California Department of Social Services within 90 days</b>
  of the denial notice <Cite>DHCS Fair Hearing Procedures, Undivided Medi-Cal Appeals Guide</Cite>.
  Your Regional Center service coordinator can often fix a miscounted case without a hearing.
</AnswerFirst>

A denial that cites your income is, in a deeming case, usually a processing error rather than
a real decision — the fix is getting the case routed to someone who knows the waiver. Put the
correction request in writing, copy your service coordinator, and calendar the appeal deadline
from the notice as your backstop. **To file an appeal:** submit a State Fair Hearing request to
California Department of Social Services within 90 days of the denial notice. File online at
<a href="https://acms.dss.ca.gov">acms.dss.ca.gov</a>, by phone at <a href="tel:1-800-743-8525">1-800-743-8525</a>, or by mail to the State Hearings Division. **To keep coverage during the appeal:** request
"aid paid pending appeal" within 10 days of the denial notice. If you want help beyond the
Regional Center, the same free advocacy resources in our
<a href="/guides/regional-centers/">Regional Center guide</a> apply here.
