<h2 class="qa-h" id="overview">Application Overview</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  You apply through your <b>county IHSS office</b>, then submit two forms: <b>SOC 873</b> (the
  IHSS health care certification every applicant needs, signed by your child's physician) and
  <b>SOC 821</b> (the protective supervision assessment your child's physician completes). A
  county social worker then visits your home to assess need and awards hours — up to the
  monthly maximum of <b>195</b>, or <b>283</b> if your child is severely impaired.
</AnswerFirst>

The process takes **8–11 weeks** from application to receiving your hours. Each step below tells you what to do, what to expect, and how long it typically takes.

---

<h2 class="qa-h" id="step-1">① Confirm Medi-Cal</h2>

**Why this matters:** IHSS requires Medi-Cal. If Medi-Cal is inactive or denied because of household income, IHSS won't happen.

**What to do:**
1. Call your county DHCS office and confirm your child's Medi-Cal status is active
2. If income was cited as a reason for denial: Ask specifically, "Can you explain institutional deeming and whether it might apply to my child?"
3. Write down your child's Medi-Cal number (you'll need it for the IHSS application)

**Timeline:** Same day or next business day

---

<h2 class="qa-h" id="step-2">② Apply to Your County IHSS Office</h2>

**Why this matters:** The IHSS office needs to know you're specifically applying for *protective supervision*. Using those exact words routes your case to the right team.

**What to do:**
1. Find your county IHSS office:
   - Search "[Your County] IHSS office" online
   - Or call 211 for local resources
   - Or visit <a href="https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/MediCal/Pages/IHSS-Offices.aspx">DHCS IHSS offices directory</a>
2. **Call and say clearly:** "I want to apply for IHSS **protective supervision** for my child" (use those exact words — it routes correctly)
3. Alternative: If your county accepts online applications, use their portal
4. Ask them to mail or email the application forms to you
5. Write down your case number when they give it

**What to expect:** Brief screening questions about your child's needs; confirmation that forms are being sent; a case number for your records

**Timeline:** 5–10 business days to receive forms by mail

---

<h2 class="qa-h" id="step-3">③ Submit SOC 873 + SOC 821</h2>

**Why this matters:** These forms tell the county your child has a medical need for protective supervision and describe exactly what that need is.

#### SOC 873 (Health Care Certification)

1. Get a blank form from the county or download from their website
2. Schedule an appointment with your child's doctor (pediatrician, developmental pediatrician, or provider who knows your child's disability)
3. Bring the form to the appointment and ask the doctor to complete and sign it
4. <a href="/resources/sample-doctor-request-for-assessment-of-need-ihss-3130">Use our sample letter</a> if you want to send a formal request first
5. Collect the signed form; you'll submit it with SOC 821

#### SOC 821 (Protective Supervision Supplemental Form)

1. Get a blank SOC 821 (included in your application packet) or download from county website
2. Write down 3–5 specific examples of times your child was about to get hurt and you had to intervene (with dates if you can remember)
3. Fill out the form clearly, using those examples
4. Describe specific safety risks (climbing, wandering, eating unsafe items, running into traffic, accessing hazardous materials)
5. Estimate hours needed per day

#### Before You Submit — Start Your Supervision Log NOW

**This is critical.** A supervision log is your strongest evidence. Even before you submit the forms, begin writing down every day:

1. **Time**
2. **What your child was doing or trying to do**
3. **What danger would have happened if you hadn't intervened**
4. **What you did to stop it**

**Example entries:**
> *9:15am: Found son standing on bathroom sink, reaching for medicine cabinet. Lifted him down, moved ladder out of house.*
>
> *2:45pm: Daughter tried to open front door to run into the street. She doesn't understand car danger. Redirected her, locked door.*
>
> *6:30am: Son eating cat food from pantry. Removed him, closed door.*

Two weeks of daily entries tells the assessor more than an hour of conversation can. Keep the log going — you'll use it during your home visit and especially if you appeal.

**What to do:**
1. Fill out both forms clearly
2. Start your supervision log immediately (keep it running)
3. Submit both SOC 873 and SOC 821 together to your county IHSS office (by mail, online portal, or in person)
4. Ask for confirmation of receipt or tracking number

**Timeline:** 2–3 weeks to prepare and submit (waiting for doctor signature may take longest)

---

<h2 class="qa-h" id="step-4">④ Home Assessment Visit</h2>

**Why this matters:** This is the county's chance to see firsthand whether your child truly needs constant supervision. Your honest, specific answers matter.

**What to expect:**
- The social worker calls to schedule (usually 30–45 days after you submit forms)
- Visit lasts 30–45 minutes
- They observe your child playing, moving around, interacting
- They ask detailed questions about what a typical day looks like

**What to do before the visit:**
1. Write down 4–5 real examples from the past week: what your child was about to do, what you had to do to stop it
2. Have your supervision log visible and up to date (they may ask to see it; don't read it aloud, just show you're tracking)
3. Clean up obvious safety hazards (unlocked doors, accessible medication, etc.) — but don't hide the normal home
4. Make sure your child is well-rested and fed so they're acting like their typical self

**What to do during the visit:**
1. Answer questions honestly and directly
2. Use specific, concrete language:
   - ✗ "My child needs supervision all the time"
   - ✓ "Last Tuesday, I found them on the roof. Yesterday, they grabbed cleaning supplies. This morning, they were trying to open the front door to run into the street. These things happen almost daily."
3. Let the social worker observe your child naturally — don't perform or coach them
4. Ask clarifying questions if you don't understand something

**Timeline:** ~30–45 days after you submit forms (timelines vary by county)

---

<h2 class="qa-h" id="step-5">⑤ Hours Awarded</h2>

**What you'll receive:**
- A letter stating the hours approved (up to 195/month, or 283 if severely impaired)
- The county's reasoning and how they calculated it
- **Your appeal rights** (usually 30 days from the letter date to file an appeal if you disagree)

**What to do:**
1. Read the letter carefully and check the math
2. Call the county to ask: "How did you arrive at this number of hours?"
3. If you agree with the hours: call to set up your provider (you, a family member, or someone else) and payment schedule
4. **If you disagree**, see the Appeal section below

**Timeline:** Usually 20–30 days after your home visit

---

<h2 class="qa-h" id="appeal">What if the County Low-Balls the Hours?</h2>

You can push back, and families regularly win on appeal. The county's hours decision is not final. If you believe the awarded hours don't match your child's actual supervision needs, you have **30 days to file for a state hearing**.

### Step 1: Request the County's Written Assessment

**Why this matters:** Their assessment shows you exactly what they think your child needs and why they didn't award more hours. This becomes the foundation of your appeal.

**What to do:**
1. Send a letter or email to your county IHSS office:
   > "Please provide the assessment and hour calculation for my child's IHSS protective supervision award dated [date from your decision letter]."
2. Keep a copy for yourself
3. Ask for confirmation of receipt
4. Wait for their response (typically 5–10 business days)

---

### Step 2: File for a State Hearing (Within 30 Days)

**Why this matters:** You're formally asking for an independent review of the county's decision by someone outside the county office.

**What to do:**
1. Check the date on your award decision letter. You have **30 days from that date** to appeal.
2. Call your county IHSS office and say: "I want to request a state hearing to appeal this IHSS decision."
3. They'll explain the process and mail you a hearing notice with your hearing date, location/dial-in number, and deadline to submit evidence
4. Write down all deadlines and put them in your calendar

**What to expect:**
- Hearing typically happens 30–60 days after you file
- You may appear in person or by phone
- You'll tell your child's story; the county will tell theirs
- An administrative law judge will listen and make a decision
- You may have legal representation, but it's not required

**Timeline:** 30–60 days from filing to hearing

---

### Step 3: Build Your Case With Concrete Evidence

**What wins appeals:** Specific, documented evidence of your child's actual supervision needs.

**Your strongest piece of evidence: Your supervision log**

A strong log shows:
- Two weeks (or more) of daily entries
- An entry for every intervention (or most of them)
- Each entry includes: date, time, what happened, what danger you prevented, what you did
- A pattern of constant need for supervision

**Example log entries:**
> *Sept 3, 9:15am: Child found standing on bathroom sink, reaching for medicine cabinet. I lifted them down, moved ladder out of house.*
>
> *Sept 3, 1:30pm: Child tried to climb out the back gate. I redirected and locked the latch.*
>
> *Sept 3, 6:00pm: Child grabbed scissors and was heading toward baby sibling. I removed scissors and redirected.*

**Other evidence that strengthens your appeal:**
1. A letter from your child's doctor describing their disability and safety needs
2. Input from your <a href="/guides/regional-centers/">Regional Center</a> service coordinator (they can write a letter supporting your assessment)
3. Photos or videos of safety hazards in your home (if they help show why supervision is needed)
4. Documentation of any injuries or near-misses (medical records, incident reports, photos)
5. Descriptions of your child's developmental level and how it compares to typical children their age

**What to do before the hearing:**
1. Organize your supervision log — make it easy to reference during the hearing
2. Write a one-page summary of your child's supervision needs (2–3 key examples)
3. Gather supporting documents (doctor's letter, RC coordinator's input, photos)
4. Prepare 3–4 specific stories you can tell about a typical day and the supervision your child needs
5. Practice explaining these clearly and factually (administrative judges respond to facts, not sentiment)

**At the hearing:**
1. Tell your story clearly and specifically
2. Use your log and documentation to back it up
3. Answer the judge's questions directly
4. Don't exaggerate — stick to what you know and have documented
5. Bring extra copies of your evidence to give to the judge

**Timeline:** 2–4 weeks after the hearing, you'll receive the judge's decision by mail
