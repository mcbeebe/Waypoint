<AnswerFirst>
  San Diego Regional Center (SDRC) serves <b>San Diego and Imperial counties</b> — one of
  California's <b>21 regional centers</b> operating under the Lanterman Act
  <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, free to your family at any income. Unlike most centers, SDRC
  routes intake <b>by your child's age</b>, not by where you live: <b>under 3</b> goes to Early
  Start intake, <b>3 and older</b> to standard intake, each with its own phone number and email
  address. For age 3 and up you can also start with an <b>online initial contact form</b>.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="is-this-my-center">Is San Diego Regional Center mine?</h2>

If you live in **San Diego county or Imperial county**, yes — those two counties are SDRC's
whole territory, and no other center covers them. Everything north and east of that belongs to
someone else: Riverside and San Bernardino to
<GatedLink href="/regional-centers/inland/">Inland Regional Center</GatedLink>, Orange county to
the <GatedLink href="/regional-centers/orange-county/">Regional Center of Orange County</GatedLink>.

Two counties is a tidy sentence that hides a lot of desert. Imperial county sits about two hours
east of the coast, over the mountains, and it is the part of SDRC's territory where "just come
in" costs a family the most. SDRC does run an office in **El Centro** — if you are in Imperial
county, ask for that office by name rather than accepting an appointment in San Diego.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="how-intake-starts">How does intake start here — and what makes SDRC different?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Age decides your door. For a child <b>under 3</b>, you contact SDRC's <b>Early Start</b> intake
  team. For a child <b>3 or older</b>, you contact standard intake — or submit SDRC's online
  <b>Initial Contact Form</b>, which is offered in several languages. Intake then reviews what
  you sent and contacts you to schedule the interview.
</AnswerFirst>

Both routes are on one page, with the current numbers and email addresses:

<a class="link" href="https://www.sdrc.org/apply">San Diego Regional Center — Apply →</a>

Two things worth doing on that page. First, **use the form if you cannot take a daytime call** —
a submission at 10pm starts the same legal clock a phone call does, and it leaves you a
timestamp. Second, **note the language options**. A form completed in the language you actually
think in produces a better description of your child than one you translate on the fly.

SDRC also has more offices than DDS's listing shows. The state directory lists the Ruffin Road
headquarters; the center's own contact page lists offices across the county and in Imperial
Valley:

<h2 class="qa-h" id="the-clocks">What is SDRC on the clock for?</h2>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>15 working days</b> from your request to initial intake <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>120 days</b> from intake to a completed assessment — <b>60 days</b> where delay would risk
  your child's health, safety, or development <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. Under 3: <b>45 days</b>
  from referral for the Early Start evaluation and assessment <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Then
  <b>60 days</b> from the completed assessment to the written plan <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

These are statewide clocks, and they run from **the date you asked**, not the date a file was
opened. Write that date down the moment you call or submit the form. It is the single most
useful thing in your folder later.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="what-to-have-ready">What should I have ready?</h2>

Bring what exists, not what you wish existed. A diagnosis is helpful and **not required** to
start — SDRC does its own assessments at no cost to you.

- Any evaluation you already have: developmental, speech, occupational therapy, psychological
- Pediatrician notes, referrals, or screening results
- Preschool, daycare, or school records; an IEP or IFSP if there is one
- Your written description of a **hard day**, with specifics: how many words, how many hours of
  sleep, how many people it takes to manage a bath

For a child **3 or older**, eligibility turns on a "substantial disability" — significant
functional limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas** <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. For a child **under
5**, there is a second route worth naming out loud: **provisional eligibility**, which needs
significant limits in **at least 2** of those areas instead of 3 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. If a
worker says your 3-year-old is close but not there, ask whether provisional eligibility was
considered.

<EmpathyCallout label="If you're in Imperial County">
  The drive to San Diego is not a detail — it is a workday, gas, and a child in a car seat over
  a mountain pass. You are allowed to say that. Ask for the El Centro office, ask whether the
  intake interview can happen by phone or video, and ask it before the first appointment is set,
  not after you have already made the drive once.
</EmpathyCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-they-say-no">What if San Diego Regional Center says no?</h2>

A denial must arrive as a written **Notice of Action**. A "we don't fund that" over the phone is
not a decision and starts no clock — ask for the written notice. From it you have **60 days** to
request a hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. Read the dates printed on your own notice and
calendar them the day it lands.

You can get free help. The **Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability
Rights California**, provides advocates who handle regional center appeals statewide, including
San Diego and Imperial counties.

<GatedLink class="link" href="/guides/regional-centers/appeal-denial/">How to appeal a Regional Center denial →</GatedLink>

You picked the right door for your child's age, dated the request, and know what a real decision
has to look like. That is the part most families learn the slow way.
