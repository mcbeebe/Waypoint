<PlainBox>
  Far Northern Regional Center arranges and pays for disability services across nine counties in
  the top of California, from the Sacramento Valley to the Oregon border. It is free, whatever
  you earn. There are two offices — Redding and Chico — and you can start intake by phone, by
  fax, or through an online request form. The clocks in California law are the same for you as
  for a family in Los Angeles.
</PlainBox>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="who">Who does Far Northern Regional Center serve?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Far Northern serves families in <b>Butte, Glenn, Lassen, Modoc, Plumas, Shasta, Siskiyou,
  Tehama, and Trinity counties</b> — nine counties across the top of the state, and one of the 21
  regional center territories created by the Lanterman Act <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite> Services are
  provided regardless of your income, and where you live is what decides which center is yours.
</AnswerFirst>

The state's listing and Far Northern's own eligibility page name exactly the same nine counties,
which is worth saying because county lists elsewhere sometimes disagree between sources. Here
they do not.

What that map means in practice: this territory runs from Chico and Oroville up through Redding,
out to Weaverville in the Coast Ranges, and across the northeast corner to Alturas and the
Oregon line. Much of it is rural, and a good deal of it is mountain. The law that applies to
your child is identical to the law in San Francisco. The driving is not.

If you are near the southern edge — Colusa, Sutter, Yuba, or Sierra county — you may belong to
a neighboring center instead. Check with our
<a href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a> before you call.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="start">How do I start intake here?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Far Northern offers <b>three ways in</b>: an intake inquiry line, a fax line, and an online
  <b>"Submit an Intake request"</b> form on its website. Any of the three starts your child's
  file. You do not need a diagnosis, a referral from a doctor, or a lawyer.
</AnswerFirst>

For a family two hours from the nearest office, the online form is the quietest of the three —
it works at 11pm, it does not require you to explain yourself to a stranger while your child is
awake, and it creates a time stamp you can point to later. Use whichever one you will actually
finish tonight.

Eligibility for children 3 and older turns on **substantial disability**: significant functional
limits in **3 or more** major life areas, from a disability that began before age 18
<Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. For children under 3, Early Start uses broader criteria — a measured
delay, an established risk condition, or a combination of high-risk factors — and no diagnosis
is needed.

<a class="link" href="https://www.farnorthernrc.org/clients/eligibility/">Far Northern: eligibility and how to request intake →</a>

1. **Send the request, and write down the date.** Every clock below runs from it. Ask for
   written confirmation — an email reply is enough.
2. **Send what you already have.** Pediatrician notes, evaluations, an IEP or IFSP, preschool or
   daycare observations, and your own written account of a hard day. Do not wait for a complete
   file.
3. **Ask, on that first contact, where the interview will happen.** This is the question that
   matters most in a nine-county territory, and it is better asked at the start than discovered
   later. [TBC: whether Far Northern offers home or remote intake interviews for outlying
   counties — it is not stated on its published pages, so ask directly rather than assuming
   either way.]
4. **Get every decision in writing.** Eligible means moving on to the Individual Program Plan
   (IPP) <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>. Not eligible means you are owed a written **Notice of Action**
   giving the reason and your appeal rights.

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>Initial intake: 15 working days</b> from your request <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>Assessment: 120 days</b> from intake — <b>60 days</b> where delay would put your child at
  risk <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. <b>Under 3: 45 days</b> from referral for evaluation and the
  first plan. <b>IPP: 60 days</b> after the assessment is complete <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

Distance is not a legal excuse for missing any of those. If a deadline passes with no decision,
say so in writing: name the date your request went in, cite <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>, and ask for
a date by which you will have a decision.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="offices">Which office will I deal with?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Far Northern runs offices in <b>Redding</b> (headquarters) and <b>Chico</b>. Its contact page
  says both serve the whole nine-county region rather than splitting the counties between them,
  so ask on your first call which one your case is assigned to — and get the name of your
  service coordinator.
</AnswerFirst>

[TBC: whether Far Northern assigns cases to Redding or Chico by county or by caseload — the
published pages do not say.]

Two practical notes for a rural territory. First, write down every name and date; when the drive
is long, most of your contact will be by phone, and phone contact leaves no paper trail unless
you make one. A short email after each call — "Confirming what we discussed today" — is the
cheapest documentation there is. Second, the process must be conducted in your preferred
language and the plan given to you in it <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>. Far Northern publishes its
family guides in English, Spanish, Korean, Tagalog, Vietnamese, and both written forms of
Chinese; if your language is not on the list, ask anyway, because the duty is to your language,
not to a menu.

Provider supply is the honest difference in a rural territory. A service your child is entitled
to still has to be delivered by someone within reach. That is a real constraint — and it is a
reason to put your request in writing, not a reason to treat "there's nobody up here" as the end
of it. If that is the answer you get, ask for it in writing, along with what the center proposes
to do instead.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="appeal">What if Far Northern says no?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Ask for the written <b>Notice of Action</b> — a "no" said on the phone is not a decision and
  starts no clock. From the day you receive the notice you have <b>60 days</b> to request an
  informal meeting, mediation, or a fair hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. If a service your
  child already gets is being cut and you want it to keep running while you appeal, that request
  must be in within <b>30 days</b> of receiving the notice <Cite>WIC §4715</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

Write both dates on the notice the day it arrives. Then get free help: the **Office of Clients'
Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability Rights California**, has advocates who handle
Lanterman Act appeals for families at no cost — and they work by phone, which matters when the
nearest office is a mountain pass away.
