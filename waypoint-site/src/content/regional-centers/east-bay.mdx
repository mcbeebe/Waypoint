<AnswerFirst>
  RCEB serves families in <b>Alameda and Contra Costa counties</b> — one of California's
  <b> 21 regional centers</b> providing services under the Lanterman Act
  <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, regardless of your income. Once intake begins, RCEB owes you
  any needed assessment within <b>120 days</b> of intake <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite> — or, if
  your child is under 3, a completed Early Start evaluation and initial family service
  plan within <b>45 days</b> of the referral <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Write down the date of your first
  contact. The clock starts then.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="intake">How does intake work at RCEB?</h2>

You can start by phone or online intake form [TBC: verify RCEB's current intake channels and
add contact details once confirmed against rceb.org]. Either way, the process runs the same. A
formal diagnosis helps but is **not required to start** — eligibility turns on "substantial
disability," meaning significant functional limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas**
<Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.

1. **Contact intake and say your child's age.** Under 3 routes to Early Start and the faster
   45-day clock; 3 and over goes through standard Lanterman intake.
2. **Gather what you have** — pediatrician notes, any evaluations or diagnosis reports, IEP or
   preschool records, insurance card. Missing pieces should not delay your request.
3. **Do the intake interview.** Describe a hard day, not your child's best day. Assessors need
   the real picture of functional limits.
4. **Note the deadline.** The assessment is due within 120 days of intake
   <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite> — under age 3, Early Start completes everything within 45 days
   of the referral <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Put it on your calendar the day you first call.
5. **Get the decision in writing.** If eligible, planning moves to your child's IPP
   <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>. If denied, you must receive a written **Notice of Action (NOA)**
   with your appeal rights.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="rceb-appeal">How do I appeal an RCEB decision?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Every denial or service reduction comes with a written <b>Notice of Action</b> — and the NOA
  is your appeal ticket, not the end of the road. Deadlines are short, so act on the date
  printed on the notice. Disagreements about the IPP itself also have a resolution path
  <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

The appeal process is the same at all 21 regional centers, so we keep the full walkthrough —
routes, timelines, and what to say at each stage — in one place.
