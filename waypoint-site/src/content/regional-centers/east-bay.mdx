<PlainBox>
  Regional Center of the East Bay (RCEB) arranges and pays for disability services in Alameda
  and Contra Costa counties. It is free, whatever you earn. The one thing to get right on your
  first call: RCEB has <b>two separate intake doors</b> — one for children under 3, one for
  age 3 and up. Knowing your child's age band before you dial saves you weeks.
</PlainBox>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="who">Who does RCEB serve?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  RCEB serves families living in <b>Alameda and Contra Costa counties</b> — Oakland, Fremont,
  Hayward, Berkeley, Concord, Richmond, and every community in between. It is one of the 21
  regional center territories created by the Lanterman Act <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>
  Services are provided regardless of your income, and your home address is what decides which
  center is yours.
</AnswerFirst>

Two counties is a small territory by California standards, and it makes RCEB unusually
navigable: one main office in Alameda County, one in Contra Costa County, and no ambiguity
about which regional center you belong to if you live in the East Bay. If you live just over a
county line — in Solano, Napa, San Joaquin, or across a bridge — you belong to a different
center, so check first with our
<a href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a>.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="two-doors">Which intake door is mine — Early Start or over 3?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  RCEB splits intake by age, and the two routes do not share a queue. <b>Birth to 3</b> goes to
  the <b>Early Start intake line</b>, which has its own phone number, its own email inbox, and
  its own fax. <b>Age 3 and older</b> goes to the <b>Intake Coordinator</b> for over-three
  services, with a different number and inbox. Both routes also accept an online inquiry form.
</AnswerFirst>

This is the most practical local fact on this page. A message left on the wrong line does not
usually bounce — it gets forwarded, eventually, and "eventually" is the enemy here. So before
you call, know two things: your child's age today, and which door that puts you at.

The current numbers, addresses, and inquiry form all live on RCEB's own eligibility page. We
link rather than reprint them, because a number we copy today is a number that can be wrong
next spring, and the main line at the top of this page will always transfer you.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="intake">How does intake work, step by step?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Say this: <i>"I'd like to request an intake and assessment for my child."</i> No diagnosis, no
  doctor's referral, and no lawyer is required to start. For children 3 and older, eligibility
  turns on <b>substantial disability</b> — significant functional limits in <b>3 or more</b>
  major life areas, from a disability that began before age 18 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. Under 3,
  Early Start uses broader criteria and no label is needed.
</AnswerFirst>

1. **Contact the right intake route and record the date.** That date starts every clock below.
   Ask for written confirmation of it — an email reply is enough.
2. **Gather what you have.** Pediatrician notes, evaluation or diagnostic reports, an IEP or
   IFSP, preschool or daycare observations, and your own written list of daily struggles. A
   missing document is not a reason to wait.
3. **Do the intake interview honestly.** Describe your hardest days, not your child's best
   ones. Assessors are measuring function across those major life areas — self-care, language,
   learning, mobility, self-direction, independent living, economic self-sufficiency.
4. **Ask for the decision in writing.** If your child is found eligible, you move to the
   Individual Program Plan (IPP), which the law says must be completed within 60 days of the
   assessment being finished <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>. If not, you are owed a written
   **Notice of Action** with the reason and your appeal rights.

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>Initial intake: 15 working days</b> from your request <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>Assessment: 120 days</b> from intake — <b>60 days</b> where delay would put your child at
  risk <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. <b>Under 3: 45 days</b> from referral for evaluation and the
  first plan. <b>IPP: 60 days</b> after the assessment is complete <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="offices">Where will meetings actually happen?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  RCEB's main office is in <b>San Leandro</b> (Alameda County) and it runs a second office in
  <b>Concord</b> (Contra Costa County). Which one you deal with generally follows which county
  you live in. Ask at intake and write down the office and your coordinator's name.
</AnswerFirst>

RCEB's eligibility page lists only the San Leandro address for intake, so it's not clear from
published sources whether Contra Costa families do intake through Concord or through San
Leandro — confirm which one applies to you when you call.

Meetings are your meetings. You can bring anyone you like — a partner, a friend, a relative, an
advocate — and you can ask for the meeting to be held in your preferred language. That last one
is a legal duty, not a favor: the planning process must be conducted in your language, and the
plan must be given to you in it <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>. RCEB publishes its information packets
in Spanish, Korean, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Tagalog, and Vietnamese, and points
families to the state for more.

Be careful with the word "local" when a coordinator uses it. "That's not something we fund
here" is a statewide claim dressed as a local one, and the Lanterman Act does not let a center
run out of an entitlement. If you hear it, ask for it in writing.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="appeal">What if RCEB says no?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Ask for the <b>Notice of Action</b> in writing — a "no" on the phone is not a decision and
  starts no clock. From the day you receive that notice, you have <b>60 days</b> to request an
  informal meeting, mediation, or a fair hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. If a service your
  child already receives is being reduced or stopped and you want it to continue during the
  appeal, that request must be in within <b>30 days</b> of receiving the notice
  <Cite>WIC §4715</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

Two dates, written on the notice itself, the day it arrives. Then call for free help: the
**Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability Rights California**, staffs
advocates who take Lanterman Act appeals for families at no charge.
