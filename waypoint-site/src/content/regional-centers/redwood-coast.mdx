<article class="prose">

<AnswerFirst>
  Redwood Coast Regional Center (RCRC) serves <b>Del Norte, Humboldt, Mendocino, and Lake
  counties</b> — one of California's <b>21 regional centers</b> funded under the Lanterman Act
  <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite> to coordinate services for people with developmental
  disabilities, regardless of your income. Intake here is routed <b>by your county and your
  child's age</b>, and RCRC runs offices in <b>Ukiah, Eureka, Crescent City, Lakeport,
  Clearlake, and Fort Bragg</b> so that most families are not driving the length of the
  territory.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="is-this-my-center">Is Redwood Coast my regional center?</h2>

If you live in **Del Norte, Humboldt, Mendocino, or Lake county**, yes. Those four counties are
RCRC's entire territory, and nobody else covers them. Unlike Los Angeles — where seven centers
carve up one county by neighborhood — the north coast boundary is simply the county line.

Just over that line, it changes. Trinity, Shasta, Siskiyou, Tehama, and Glenn counties belong
to <GatedLink href="/regional-centers/far-northern/">Far Northern Regional Center</GatedLink>.
Sonoma, Napa, and Solano belong to
<GatedLink href="/regional-centers/north-bay/">North Bay Regional Center</GatedLink>. Colusa and
Yolo belong to Alta California. If you moved recently, or you live in one county and your child
attends school in another, the rule is your **home address**, not the school. Our
<a class="link" href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a> settles it in
a few seconds.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="how-intake-starts">How does intake actually start here?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  RCRC splits the front door two ways: <b>by county</b> and <b>by your child's age</b>. For a
  child <b>under 3</b>, you call your county's Early Start intake department directly. For
  <b>3 and older</b>, you leave a message on your county's recorded referral line — there are
  separate extensions for <b>English and Spanish</b> — and RCRC then <b>mails you an intake
  inquiry packet</b> to complete and send back.
</AnswerFirst>

That mailed packet is the part that surprises people. It means the clock does not depend on you
reaching a human on the first try, but it does mean **your mailing address has to be right and
the envelope has to come back**. Two practical moves: when you leave the referral message, say
your address slowly and twice, and write down the date you called. And when the packet arrives,
photograph every page before you mail it. Rural mail goes missing, and a photo is proof of what
you sent and when.

The packet is available in **English, Spanish, Hmong, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional
Chinese**. If your family reads a different language, ask for interpretation at the intake
interview — you are entitled to be understood, not just addressed.

RCRC's own apply-for-services page walks through both routes and lists the county numbers and
extensions:

<a class="link" href="https://redwoodcoastrc.org/for-clients/apply-for-services/">Redwood Coast — Apply for Services →</a>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="the-clocks">What deadlines is RCRC actually on?</h2>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>15 working days</b> from your request to initial intake <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>120 days</b> from intake to a completed assessment — <b>60 days</b> if delay would put
  your child's health, safety, or development at risk <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. Under age 3:
  <b>45 days</b> from referral for the Early Start evaluation and assessment
  <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Then <b>60 days</b> from the completed assessment to your child's
  first written plan <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

RCRC's own site tells families the intake process "can take up to 120 days" once the initial
interview is scheduled. That matches the statute, and it is worth reading carefully: 120 days is
the **outer limit**, not the schedule. If your child is at risk of losing a placement, or is
regressing while you wait, say so in writing and ask for the 60-day track by name
<Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>.

<EmpathyCallout label="If the distance is the problem">
  Up here, "just come in for the meeting" can mean a two-hour drive each way on a two-lane road,
  in winter, with a child who does not travel well. That is a real barrier, and it is fair to
  say so out loud. Ask directly: can this interview happen by phone or video? Can we meet at the
  office closest to me instead of the one that opened my file? A no is possible. Not asking
  guarantees the drive.
</EmpathyCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="whats-the-same">What is the same everywhere — and what is local?</h2>

**The same at all 21 centers:** who qualifies, the clocks above, and your appeal rights.
Eligibility at 3 and older turns on a developmental disability that began before 18 and is a
"substantial disability" — significant functional limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas**
<Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. A child **under 5** can also be found *provisionally* eligible on a
lower bar: significant limits in **at least 2** of those areas <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. A
diagnosis is not required to start.

**Local to RCRC:** which office answers, the county-and-age routing, the mailed packet, the
languages the packet comes in, and how far you travel. None of that changes what your child is
entitled to. It only changes the logistics you plan around.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-they-say-no">What if Redwood Coast says no?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  A denial has to arrive as a written <b>Notice of Action</b>. A phone call saying "she probably
  won't qualify" is not a decision and starts no clock — ask for it in writing. From the written
  notice you have <b>60 days</b> to request a hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. Check the dates
  printed on your own notice, and calendar them the day it arrives.
</AnswerFirst>

You do not have to do this alone or pay anyone. The **Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy
(OCRA)**, part of **Disability Rights California**, provides free advocates who handle regional
center appeals statewide, including the north coast. Denials get reversed regularly.

<GatedLink class="link" href="/guides/regional-centers/appeal-denial/">How to appeal a Regional Center denial →</GatedLink>

You made the call, wrote down the date, and know which clock you are on. That is more than most
families have on day one.
