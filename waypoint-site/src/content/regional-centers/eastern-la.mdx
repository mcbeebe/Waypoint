<h2 class="qa-h" id="who">Which Los Angeles families does ELARC serve?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  ELARC serves part of <b>Los Angeles County</b> — the state lists its area as the
  <b>health districts of Alhambra, East Los Angeles, Northeast, and Whittier</b>. Los Angeles
  County is served by <b>seven</b> different regional centers, so being in the county is not
  enough to tell you which one is yours. Your home address is.
</AnswerFirst>

Health districts are county public-health boundaries, not city limits, and most families have
never heard of them — which is exactly why this trips people up. A district name is not a
compass direction, and it does not map cleanly onto the neighborhood name you use for your own
block. Two families a few streets apart can belong to two different regional centers.

So do not guess from a district name you half-recognize. Check your address:

<h2 class="qa-h" id="wrong-center">What if I called and I'm at the wrong center?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Nothing is lost. Regional centers refer families to the correct territory, and being at the
  wrong door does not restart your child's eligibility. But it does cost time, so it is worth
  ten seconds of checking before you spend an hour on the phone.
</AnswerFirst>

If ELARC turns out not to be yours, the other centers serving Los Angeles County are San
Gabriel/Pomona, Frank D. Lanterman, North Los Angeles County, South Central Los Angeles,
Westside, and Harbor. Each has its own page here, with its own intake details:

<h2 class="qa-h" id="apply">How does intake start at ELARC?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  ELARC runs intake through a written <b>application</b>, and which one you fill out depends on
  your child's age. Birth to 3 uses the <b>Early Start Program application</b>, handled by
  ELARC's Early Start Intake Department. Age 3 and above uses the <b>General Intake
  application</b>, handled by the Intake &amp; Assessment Unit. Two different forms, two
  different teams, two different legal clocks.
</AnswerFirst>

You do not need a diagnosis, a doctor's referral, or a lawyer to submit either one. For children
3 and older, eligibility turns on **substantial disability** — significant functional limits in
**3 or more** major life areas, from a disability that began before age 18 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.
Under 3, Early Start uses broader criteria: a measured developmental delay, an established risk
condition, or a combination of high-risk factors.

1. **Get the right application** from ELARC's apply page, and note the date you submit it. That
   date starts every clock below.
2. **Attach what you already have** — pediatrician notes, any evaluation or diagnostic report,
   an IEP or IFSP, preschool observations. Do not hold the application back waiting for a
   missing document.
3. **Write your own account of a hard day.** One page, concrete, unpolished. It is often the
   most useful document in the file, because it describes function, which is what the law
   measures.
4. **Ask for every decision in writing.** Eligible means moving to the Individual Program Plan
   (IPP) <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>. Not eligible means you are owed a written **Notice of Action**
   stating the reason and your appeal rights.

<a class="link" href="https://www.elarc.org/People-We-Serve/Apply-for-Services">ELARC: Apply for Services →</a>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>Initial intake: 15 working days</b> from your request <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>Assessment: 120 days</b> from intake — <b>60 days</b> where delay would put your child at
  risk <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. <b>Under 3: 45 days</b> from referral for evaluation and the
  first plan. <b>IPP: 60 days</b> after the assessment is complete <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="offices">Where is ELARC, and what language will this happen in?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  ELARC's main office is in <b>Alhambra</b>, with a satellite office in <b>Whittier</b>. And the
  whole process — meetings, planning, the written plan itself — must happen in
  <b>your preferred language</b>. That is a legal duty on the regional center
  <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>, not a courtesy you have to earn.
</AnswerFirst>

ELARC publishes its site in English, Spanish, Simplified Chinese, Vietnamese, and Korean. If
your language is not on that list, ask anyway — the statutory duty is to your preferred
language, not to a menu. Say it at the moment you submit the application, so the interpreter is
scheduled rather than improvised.

And please do not let your bilingual older child interpret a meeting about their sibling's
disability. Families do it constantly because it feels easier than asking. It is not their job,
and the law never asked it of them.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="appeal">What if ELARC says no?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Ask for the written <b>Notice of Action</b>. A "no" over the phone or in a hallway is not a
  decision and starts no clock. From the day you receive the notice, you have <b>60 days</b> to
  request an informal meeting, mediation, or a fair hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. If a
  service your child already receives is being cut and you want it to keep running during the
  appeal, that request has to be in within <b>30 days</b> of receiving the notice
  <Cite>WIC §4715</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

Write both dates on the notice the day it arrives. Then get free help — the **Office of Clients'
Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability Rights California**, has advocates who handle
Lanterman Act appeals for families at no cost, including in Spanish.
