<AnswerFirst>
  South Central Los Angeles Regional Center (SCLARC) serves the <b>Compton, San Antonio, South,
  Southeast, and Southwest health districts</b> of Los Angeles County, under the Lanterman Act
  <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, free at any family income. In plain geography, that is
  <b>South Los Angeles, Watts, Leimert Park, Florence/Firestone, West Adams</b> and the cities of
  <b>Bell, Bell Gardens, Compton, Cudahy, Downey, Gardena, Huntington Park, Lynwood, Maywood,
  Paramount, South Gate, and Vernon</b>. SCLARC takes applications through an <b>online intake
  form</b> as well as by phone.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="is-this-my-center">Is SCLARC my regional center?</h2>

In most of California, one county means one regional center. Los Angeles County is the exception:
**seven** centers split it, and they split it along **health district** boundaries — an
administrative map, not city limits and not school district lines.

That is why "I live in Los Angeles" does not answer the question. The City of Los Angeles is
carved up among several centers. What decides your case is which health district your **street
address** falls in.

SCLARC covers five of them: **Compton, San Antonio, South, Southeast, and Southwest**. If you
live in Watts, Leimert Park, Florence/Firestone, West Adams, or the southeast cities listed
above, that is SCLARC's territory.

Where it gets close, check carefully:

- **Gardena** sits near Harbor Regional Center's Torrance health district.
- **Downey** and **Paramount** sit near Harbor's Bellflower health district.
- North and west of the Southwest district, you cross into Westside and Frank D. Lanterman
  territory.

Three ways to settle it, best first: use our
<a class="link" href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a> with your full
address; check the state directory at
<a class="link" href="https://www.dds.ca.gov/rc/listings/">DDS Regional Center listings</a>; or
call SCLARC and read your street address out before anything else. Five minutes on the phone
beats six weeks of intake at the wrong center.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="how-intake-starts">How does intake start here?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  SCLARC's front door is an <b>online application form</b> — you can complete it at any hour,
  which matters if you cannot take a call between 8 and 5. You can also call the main line and
  ask for <b>intake and assessment</b>. Say your child's age first: <b>under 3</b> routes to
  Early Start and a faster clock, <b>3 and older</b> to standard Lanterman intake.
</AnswerFirst>

<a class="link" href="https://intake.sclarc.org/AppForm.aspx">SCLARC — start the online application →</a>

Whichever door you use, do one thing before you close the tab: **write down the date**. That date
is when the legal clocks below start running, and it is the fact you will need most in three
months.

SCLARC runs two offices — one on **South Western Avenue in Los Angeles** and one on **Garfield
Avenue in South Gate**. If the southeast cities are your side of the territory, ask whether your
meetings can happen at South Gate rather than crossing the county at rush hour.

**What to have ready**, without waiting for a perfect folder:

- Pediatrician notes, referrals, developmental screening results
- Any evaluation — speech, occupational therapy, psychological, hearing
- Preschool or school records, an IEP or IFSP if there is one
- Your own written description of a **hard day**: what your child cannot do yet, how often, and
  what it costs the household

A diagnosis is helpful and **not required** to begin. SCLARC funds its own assessments at no cost
to you.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="the-clocks">What deadlines are they on?</h2>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>15 working days</b> from your request to initial intake <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>120 days</b> from intake to a completed assessment — <b>60 days</b> where delay would risk
  your child's health, safety, or development <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. Under 3: <b>45 days</b>
  from referral for the Early Start evaluation and assessment <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Then
  <b>60 days</b> from the completed assessment to the written plan <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

Those clocks are the same at all 21 centers and they run from **your** request. If one passes,
say so in writing: name the date you applied, cite <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite> or
<Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>, and ask for a decision date in reply. Nothing about that is rude. It is
the deadline the law wrote for them.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="eligibility">Who qualifies?</h2>

At **3 and older**: a developmental disability that began before age 18 and is a "substantial
disability" — significant functional limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas** <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.
The seven are self-care, receptive and expressive language, learning, mobility, self-direction,
capacity for independent living, and economic self-sufficiency. A young child is measured against
what is expected at that age.

Under **age 5**, ask about **provisional eligibility** by name: it needs significant limits in
**at least 2** of those areas rather than 3, and it exists precisely for young children whose
picture is not complete yet <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-they-say-no">What if SCLARC says no?</h2>

A denial has to come as a written **Notice of Action**. An informal no on the phone is not a
decision and starts no clock — ask for the written notice every time. From it you have **60
days** to request a hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. Check the dates printed on your own notice
and calendar them the day it arrives.

Free help is available and worth using. The **Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part
of **Disability Rights California**, provides advocates who handle regional center appeals across
Los Angeles County at no cost to you.
