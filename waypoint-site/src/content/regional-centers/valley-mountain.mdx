  Amador, Calaveras, and Tuolumne up in the foothills. It coordinates and pays for services for

<AnswerFirst>
  Valley Mountain Regional Center (VMRC) serves <b>San Joaquin, Stanislaus, Amador, Calaveras,
  and Tuolumne counties</b> — one of California's <b>21 regional centers</b> under the Lanterman
  Act <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, free at any family income. Intake is <b>routed by
  county</b> to one of three branches: <b>Stockton</b> (San Joaquin), <b>Modesto</b>
  (Stanislaus), and the town of <b>San Andreas</b> (Amador, Calaveras, Tuolumne). VMRC names
  <b>three</b> eligibility doors, not two — Early Start, provisional eligibility under age 5, and
  standard Lanterman eligibility at 3 and older.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="is-this-my-center">Is Valley Mountain my regional center?</h2>

If you live in **San Joaquin, Stanislaus, Amador, Calaveras, or Tuolumne county**, yes. The
boundary is the county line, which makes this simpler than Los Angeles County, where seven
centers divide one county by neighborhood.

Two corrections worth making, because both send families to the wrong place:

- **Mariposa county is not VMRC.** It belongs to
  <GatedLink href="/regional-centers/central-valley/">Central Valley Regional Center</GatedLink>,
  along with Merced, Madera, Fresno, Kings, and Tulare. Older lists get this wrong.
- **Sacramento and the counties north of Amador are not VMRC** either — those belong to
  <GatedLink href="/regional-centers/alta-california/">Alta California Regional Center</GatedLink>.

<NoticeBox>
  <b>A confusing name collision.</b> VMRC's foothill branch is in the <b>town of San Andreas</b>,
  in Calaveras County. <b>San Andreas Regional Center</b> is a completely different organization,
  serving Santa Clara, Santa Cruz, San Benito, and Monterey counties on the coast. If you search
  "San Andreas regional center" from Angels Camp or Sonora, you will land on the wrong one. Your
  center is Valley Mountain.
</NoticeBox>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="three-doors">Which of VMRC's three doors is my child's?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  VMRC sorts by age into three tracks. <b>Under 3</b> is Early Start. <b>Under 5</b> can qualify
  through <b>provisional eligibility</b>, which uses a lower bar. <b>3 and older</b> goes through
  standard Lanterman eligibility. A child can move between them — provisional eligibility is
  reassessed before age 5 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

<div class="table-wrap">
  <table class="data">
    <tr><th>Door</th><th>Age</th><th>What it takes</th></tr>
    <tr><td>Early Start</td><td>Birth to 3</td><td>Developmental delay or an at-risk condition — broader criteria, no diagnosis required</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Provisional eligibility</td><td>Under 5</td><td>A disability that is not solely physical, with significant functional limits in <b>at least 2</b> of the 7 life areas <span class="cite">WIC §4512</span></td></tr>
    <tr><td>Lanterman eligibility</td><td>3 and older</td><td>A developmental disability beginning before age 18, with significant functional limits in <b>3 or more</b> of the 7 life areas <span class="cite">WIC §4512</span></td></tr>
  </table>
</div>

The middle row is the one worth arguing for. Many parents of 3- and 4-year-olds are told their
child is "close, but not three areas" and hear that as a final no. It is not: **provisional
eligibility asks for two areas, not three**, and it exists precisely because a young child's
picture is incomplete. A child found provisionally eligible is reassessed at least 90 days before
turning 5 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite> — so it buys real years of service, not a technicality.

The seven life areas are self-care, receptive and expressive language, learning, mobility,
self-direction, capacity for independent living, and economic self-sufficiency. A preschooler is
measured against what is expected at that age.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="how-intake-starts">How does intake start here?</h2>

Call the branch for your county and ask for **intake and assessment**, saying your child's age
first. VMRC publishes a separate intake number for each of its three branches, so calling the
right one saves a transfer:

<div class="table-wrap">
  <table class="data">
    <tr><th>Your county</th><th>VMRC branch</th></tr>
    <tr><td>San Joaquin</td><td>Stockton</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Stanislaus</td><td>Modesto</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Amador · Calaveras · Tuolumne</td><td>San Andreas (Calaveras County)</td></tr>
  </table>
</div>

Write down the date you call — that date starts every clock below. Then gather what you already
have: pediatrician notes, any evaluation (speech, occupational therapy, psychological), school or
preschool records, an IEP or IFSP if one exists, and your own written description of a **hard
day**. A diagnosis helps but is **not required** to start, and VMRC funds its own assessments at
no cost to you.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="the-clocks">What deadlines is VMRC on?</h2>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>15 working days</b> from your request to initial intake <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>120 days</b> from intake to a completed assessment — <b>60 days</b> where delay would risk
  your child's health, safety, or development <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. Under 3: <b>45 days</b>
  from referral for the Early Start evaluation and assessment <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Then
  <b>60 days</b> from the completed assessment to the written plan <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

Those clocks are identical at all 21 centers, and they run from **your** request. What varies
locally is who answers, how far you drive, and which providers have openings near you. None of
that changes what your child is owed.

<EmpathyCallout label="If you're up in the foothills">
  Highway 49 in winter, a child who does not do well in the car, and an appointment ninety
  minutes down the hill — that is a real reason families put the call off for months. Say it on
  the first call: which meetings have to be in person, which can be by phone or video, and can
  anything happen at the San Andreas branch instead of Stockton? The worst outcome of asking is
  the drive you were already making.
</EmpathyCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-they-say-no">What if Valley Mountain says no?</h2>

A denial must arrive as a written **Notice of Action** stating the decision and your appeal
rights. An informal no on the phone is not a decision and starts no clock — ask for the written
notice. From it, you have **60 days** to request a hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>; check the
dates printed on your own notice and calendar them the day it comes.

Before you appeal, ask one question in writing: **was provisional eligibility considered?** For a
child under 5, that is the most common thing a denial letter skips.

Free advocates handle these appeals. The **Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of
**Disability Rights California**, works with families statewide.

You know which branch is yours, which of the three doors fits your child, and what a real
decision has to look like on paper. Most families spend a year learning that.
