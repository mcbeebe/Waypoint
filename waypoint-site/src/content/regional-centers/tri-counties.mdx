<article class="prose">

<AnswerFirst>
  Tri-Counties Regional Center (TCRC) serves <b>San Luis Obispo, Santa Barbara, and Ventura
  counties</b> — one of California's <b>21 regional centers</b> under the Lanterman Act
  <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, free at any family income. Its territory runs roughly
  <b>200 miles</b> of coast, so TCRC operates <b>seven offices</b>: Santa Barbara, Santa Maria,
  San Luis Obispo, Atascadero, Ventura, Simi Valley, and Fillmore. Its application asks your
  child's age first — <b>under 3</b> and <b>3 and older</b> follow different tracks.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="is-this-my-center">Is Tri-Counties my regional center?</h2>

If you live in **San Luis Obispo, Santa Barbara, or Ventura county**, yes. The boundary is the
county line — clean, unlike Los Angeles County an hour to the south, where seven centers split
one county by health district and a family's street address decides.

Around the edges: Monterey and San Benito to the north belong to
<GatedLink href="/regional-centers/san-andreas/">San Andreas Regional Center</GatedLink>; Kern,
Inyo and Mono over the mountains belong to
<GatedLink href="/regional-centers/kern/">Kern Regional Center</GatedLink>; the far side of the
Ventura county line is Los Angeles County and a different center entirely. What decides your
case is **where your child lives** — not the school district, not where the pediatrician is.
Our <a class="link" href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a> confirms it
in seconds.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="which-office">Which office should I be dealing with?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  TCRC runs <b>seven</b> offices, and that is the most useful local fact on this page. From Paso
  Robles to Fillmore is several hours of driving. Before you accept an appointment, ask: <i>"Which
  office is closest to me, and can my meetings happen there?"</i> Nobody will volunteer it.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="how-intake-starts">How does intake start here?</h2>

The application asks one question before anything else: **is the person seeking services over
age 3, or under age 3?** That single choice sets which track you are on and which clock runs.

<div class="table-wrap">
  <table class="data">
    <tr><th>Your child's age</th><th>Track</th><th>The clock</th></tr>
    <tr><td>Under 3</td><td>Early Start</td><td><b>45 days</b> from referral to the evaluation and assessment <span class="cite">17 CCR §52086</span></td></tr>
    <tr><td>3 and older</td><td>Standard Lanterman intake</td><td><b>15 working days</b> to intake <span class="cite">WIC §4642</span>, then <b>120 days</b> to a completed assessment <span class="cite">WIC §4643</span></td></tr>
  </table>
</div>

<a class="link" href="https://www.tri-counties.org/our-services/apply/">Tri-Counties — apply for services →</a>

After you apply, TCRC connects you with an **Intake Coordinator**. Get that person's name, direct
number, and email on the first contact, and write down the date you applied. On a territory this
size, the coordinator is your single point of contact, and the date is what every deadline runs
from.

**What to bring** — what exists, not a perfect folder:

- Pediatrician notes, referrals, developmental screening results
- Any evaluation: speech, occupational therapy, psychological, hearing
- Preschool or school records; an IEP or IFSP if there is one
- Your own written description of a **hard day**, in specifics

A diagnosis is helpful and **not required** to begin. TCRC funds its own assessments at no cost
to you.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="the-clocks">What are the deadlines, exactly?</h2>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>15 working days</b> from your request to initial intake <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>120 days</b> from intake to a completed assessment — <b>60 days</b> where delay would risk
  your child's health, safety, or development <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. Under 3: <b>45 days</b>
  from referral for the Early Start evaluation and assessment <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Then
  <b>60 days</b> from the completed assessment to the written plan <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

Those are statewide and they do not stretch for distance. A long drive is your logistics problem
and TCRC's scheduling problem — it is not a lawful reason for the assessment to run past 120
days. If a clock passes, write to your coordinator with the date you applied, the statute, and a
request for a decision date.

Eligibility at **3 and older**: a developmental disability that began before 18 and is a
"substantial disability" — significant functional limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas**
<Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. Under **age 5**, ask about **provisional eligibility** by name — it
needs limits in **at least 2** of those areas rather than 3 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.

<EmpathyCallout label="When the map is the problem">
  Three counties on one page looks tidy. On the ground it is mountain passes, a two-lane
  stretch of the 101, and an appointment that eats a workday. That is a real cost and you are
  allowed to name it out loud: "the nearest office to me is X — can we meet there, or by video?"
  Asking early is the difference between one drive and four.
</EmpathyCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-they-say-no">What if Tri-Counties says no?</h2>

A denial must arrive as a written **Notice of Action** explaining the decision and your appeal
rights. A phone call saying "that's not something we fund" is not a decision and starts no
clock — ask for the written notice. From it you have **60 days** to request a hearing
<Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. Check the dates printed on your own notice.

The **Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability Rights California**,
provides free advocates who handle regional center appeals statewide. It costs nothing and it
changes the room.

<GatedLink class="link" href="/guides/regional-centers/appeal-denial/">How to appeal a Regional Center denial →</GatedLink>

You picked the right track for your child's age, found the office nearest you, and dated the
request. On a coast this long, that is the whole game.
