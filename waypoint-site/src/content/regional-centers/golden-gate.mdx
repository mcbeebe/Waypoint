<PlainBox>
  Golden Gate Regional Center (GGRC) arranges and pays for disability services in Marin, San
  Francisco, and San Mateo counties. It is free, whatever you earn. GGRC has one office per
  county and starts intake from a form: one for children under 3, a different one for age 3 and
  up. Fill in the right form, send it, and write down the date — the legal clocks start then.
</PlainBox>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="who">Who does Golden Gate Regional Center serve?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  GGRC serves families living in <b>Marin, San Francisco, and San Mateo counties</b> — one of
  the 21 regional center territories created by the Lanterman Act <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>
  Services are provided regardless of your income. Where your family lives decides which center
  is yours, not where your child goes to school or sees a doctor.
</AnswerFirst>

It is a compact territory with a long shape: from northern Marin down through San Francisco and
the length of the Peninsula to the San Mateo County line. Three counties, three very different
housing and transit realities, one set of rules.

If you live in the East Bay, in Santa Clara County, or in Sonoma or Napa, a different center
serves you. Check first with our
<a href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a> — it takes seconds and saves
a phone call you did not need to make.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="apply">How do I apply to GGRC right now?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  GGRC starts intake from a <b>downloadable referral form</b>, and which one you need depends on
  your child's age: the <b>Early Start form</b> for ages 0–36 months, or the
  <b>Lanterman Intake form</b> for ages 3 and up. As of <b>September 2026</b>, GGRC's apply page
  says its new online application is being repaired and asks families to fill out the form and
  email it to <b>intake@ggrc.org</b> in the meantime.
</AnswerFirst>

We date-stamp that last sentence on purpose. A broken online form is a temporary state, and by
the time you read this the online application may be back. Check GGRC's apply page before you
assume either way — but do not let the uncertainty stop you. An emailed PDF starts your child's
file exactly as well as a web form does, and it gives you something a web form often does not: a
sent-items copy with a date on it.

<a class="link" href="https://www.ggrc.org/apply/">GGRC: apply for services →</a>

You do not need a diagnosis, a doctor's referral, or a lawyer to send either form. For children
3 and older, eligibility turns on **substantial disability** — significant functional limits in
**3 or more** major life areas, from a disability that began before age 18 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.
Under 3, Early Start is broader: a measured developmental delay, an established risk condition,
or a combination of high-risk factors, with no label required.

1. **Download the form that matches your child's age today.** If your child turns 3 soon, ask
   GGRC which form to use rather than guessing — the two routes have different clocks.
2. **Attach what you already have** — pediatrician notes, evaluation or diagnostic reports, an
   IEP or IFSP, preschool observations. Send the form now and the rest later if you have to.
3. **Write one page in your own words** describing a hard day, start to finish. It is often the
   most useful document in the whole packet.
4. **Keep the sent email.** That is your proof of the date, and every deadline below runs from
   it.

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>Initial intake: 15 working days</b> from your request <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>Assessment: 120 days</b> from intake — <b>60 days</b> where delay would put your child at
  risk <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. <b>Under 3: 45 days</b> from referral for evaluation and the
  first plan. <b>IPP: 60 days</b> after the assessment is complete <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="offices">Which GGRC office is mine?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  GGRC runs <b>one office per county</b> — San Francisco (its main office), San Rafael for Marin
  families, and San Mateo for the Peninsula — and each has its own direct phone number. Most of
  your contact will be with your county's office rather than the headquarters.
</AnswerFirst>

That structure is a genuine convenience: you are not competing with two other counties for the
same front desk. It also means the number you find in an old email or a search result may be a
different county's office. When in doubt, call the main line at the top of this page, or take
the current numbers from GGRC's own offices page — never from a third-party listing.

<a class="link" href="https://www.ggrc.org/contact-us/our-offices/">GGRC office locations →</a>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="language">Can I do this in my language?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Yes, and it is a right rather than a favor. California law requires the regional center to
  conduct the planning process in <b>your preferred language</b> and to give you the plan in it
  <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>. GGRC says directly that it can help you complete your application and
  intake in your preferred language, and publishes its site in nine languages besides English.
</AnswerFirst>

Those are Spanish, Simplified and Traditional Chinese, Tagalog, Russian, Vietnamese, Arabic,
Korean, and Portuguese. If yours is not among them, ask anyway — the statutory duty runs to your
language, not to a published list. Ask at the moment you send the form, so an interpreter can be
scheduled rather than improvised. And do not put a bilingual older child in the position of
interpreting a meeting about their sibling's disability.

One caution specific to an expensive region: cost of living is not a lawful reason to reduce a
service, and neither is a center's budget. The Lanterman Act makes services an entitlement for
people who qualify. If you hear a version of "we can't afford that here," ask for it in writing.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="appeal">What if GGRC says no?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Ask for the written <b>Notice of Action</b>. A "no" said in a meeting is not a decision and
  starts no clock. From the day you receive the notice you have <b>60 days</b> to request an
  informal meeting, mediation, or a fair hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. If a service your
  child already gets is being cut and you want it to continue while you appeal, that request has
  to go in within <b>30 days</b> of receiving the notice <Cite>WIC §4715</Cite>.
</AnswerFirst>

Write both dates on the notice itself the day it arrives. Then get free help — the **Office of
Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability Rights California**, staffs advocates
who handle Lanterman Act appeals for families at no cost.
