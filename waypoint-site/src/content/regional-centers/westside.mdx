<AnswerFirst>
  Westside Regional Center (WRC) serves the <b>Inglewood</b> and <b>Santa Monica-West</b> health
  districts of Los Angeles County, under the Lanterman Act <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, free
  at any family income. That covers roughly <b>21 cities and neighborhoods</b> from Malibu and
  Pacific Palisades down through Santa Monica, Venice, Culver City and West LA to Inglewood,
  Hawthorne, Lawndale and El Segundo. WRC publishes <b>the ZIP codes it covers and a map</b> —
  the fastest way in Los Angeles to know whether a center is yours.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="is-this-my-center">Is Westside my regional center?</h2>

Almost everywhere in California, your county names your regional center. Los Angeles County is
split among **seven** of them, and the split follows **health districts** — an administrative map
with no street signs. So "I live in Los Angeles" answers nothing: the City of Los Angeles is
divided among several centers, and Westside holds only the coastal slice of it (Venice, Mar
Vista, Westchester, West LA, Pacific Palisades, Bel Air, Playa del Rey).

Westside makes this easier than most. Its service-area page lists the cities **and** the ZIP
codes it covers, with a downloadable map:

<a class="link" href="https://westsiderc.org/who-we-are/service-area/">Westside Regional Center — service area, ZIP list and map →</a>

Cities on Westside's own list: Bel Air · Beverly Hills · Calabasas · Culver City · El Segundo ·
Gardena · Hawthorne · Inglewood · Lawndale · Lennox · Los Angeles · Malibu · Marina del Rey ·
Mar Vista · Pacific Palisades · Playa del Rey · Santa Monica · Topanga Canyon · Venice ·
Westchester · West Los Angeles

<NoticeBox>
  <b>Check the ZIP, not the city.</b> Some of these names sit on a seam. <b>Gardena</b>, for
  example, is on Westside's list and also borders Harbor Regional Center's Torrance district and
  South Central LA's Southwest district. A city name that appears on two centers' lists is not a
  contradiction — the health district line runs through the city, and your ZIP or street address
  decides.
</NoticeBox>

If you are still unsure, three checks, best first: our
<a class="link" href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a>; the state's
directory at <a class="link" href="https://www.dds.ca.gov/rc/listings/">DDS Regional Center listings</a>;
or a call to WRC with your street address in front of you. If it is not their area, they will
name the center that is — a five-minute call instead of a six-week detour.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="how-intake-starts">How does intake start here?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Start at WRC's intake page or call the main line and say: <i>"I'd like to request an intake and
  assessment for my child for Regional Center eligibility."</i> Lead with your child's age —
  <b>under 3</b> routes to Early Start on a 45-day clock, <b>3 and older</b> to standard
  Lanterman intake. Then write down the date you contacted them. That date starts every deadline
  below.
</AnswerFirst>

<a class="link" href="https://westsiderc.org/intake/">Westside Regional Center — Intake →</a>

WRC's office is in **El Segundo**, near the airport. From Malibu or Topanga that is a serious
drive, especially at the hours schools let out. Ask on the first call whether the intake
interview can happen by phone or video — the answer varies, and asking costs nothing.

**Bring what exists, not what you wish existed:**

- Pediatrician notes, referrals, developmental screening results
- Any evaluation — speech, occupational therapy, psychological, hearing
- Preschool or school records; an IEP or IFSP if there is one
- Your own written description of a **hard day**, in specifics rather than adjectives

A diagnosis helps and is **not required** to start. The center funds its own assessments at no
cost to you, so a missing report is not a reason to wait another month.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="the-clocks">What is Westside on the clock for?</h2>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>15 working days</b> from your request to initial intake <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>120 days</b> from intake to a completed assessment — <b>60 days</b> where delay would risk
  your child's health, safety, or development <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. Under 3: <b>45 days</b>
  from referral for the Early Start evaluation and assessment <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Then
  <b>60 days</b> from the completed assessment to the written plan <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

Same at all 21 centers, and they run from **your** request rather than from whenever a file gets
opened. If a clock passes, put it in writing — the date you contacted them, the statute, and a
request for a decision date. Centers respond to parents who know the deadline.

Eligibility at **3 and older** means a developmental disability that began before 18 and is a
"substantial disability" — significant functional limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas**
<Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. Under **age 5**, ask about **provisional eligibility** by name: it needs
limits in **at least 2** of those areas instead of 3 <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. Many parents of
3- and 4-year-olds are never told that second door exists.

<EmpathyCallout label="On the map problem">
  Nobody grows up knowing what health district they live in. If you have already called one
  center and been told you belong to another, you did not do anything wrong — you ran into a
  boundary that the county drew for public health decades ago and never redrew for families. Get
  the ZIP checked once, write down which center is yours, and you never have to think about it
  again.
</EmpathyCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-they-say-no">What if Westside says no?</h2>

A denial must arrive as a written **Notice of Action**. An informal no over the phone is not a
decision and starts no clock — ask for it in writing, every time. From the written notice you
have **60 days** to request a hearing <Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>. Read the dates on your own notice
and calendar them the day it arrives.

Help is free. The **Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability Rights
California**, provides advocates who handle regional center appeals throughout Los Angeles
County at no cost to families.

<GatedLink class="link" href="/guides/regional-centers/appeal-denial/">How to appeal a Regional Center denial →</GatedLink>

You checked the ZIP instead of trusting the city name, and you dated the request. Those two
habits carry you through the rest of this system.
