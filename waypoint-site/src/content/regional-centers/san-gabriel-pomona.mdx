<AnswerFirst>
  San Gabriel/Pomona Regional Center (SG/PRC) serves about <b>30 cities</b> in the
  <b>El Monte, Foothill, and Pomona</b> areas of <b>Los Angeles County</b>, under the Lanterman
  Act <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, free to your family at any income. Los Angeles County is
  divided among <b>seven</b> regional centers along <b>health district</b> lines — not city
  lines — so several cities in this area are <b>split between two centers</b>. Confirm your
  address before you start, because starting at the wrong center costs weeks.
</AnswerFirst>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="is-this-my-center">Is San Gabriel/Pomona my regional center?</h2>

Everywhere else in California, the answer is a county. In Los Angeles it is a neighborhood — and
the neighborhood boundaries are **Los Angeles County health districts**, an administrative map
most residents have never seen and no street sign mentions.

SG/PRC's own service area lists these cities:

Altadena · Arcadia · Azusa · Baldwin Park · Charter Oak · City of Industry · Claremont · Covina ·
Diamond Bar · Duarte · El Monte · Glendora · Hacienda Heights · Irwindale · La Puente ·
La Verne · Monrovia · Pasadena · Pomona · Rowland Heights · San Dimas · Sierra Madre ·
South El Monte · Temple City · Valinda · Walnut · West Covina · Whittier

<NoticeBox>
  <b>Four of those cities are shared.</b> SG/PRC states that portions of <b>Pasadena, Pomona,
  Temple City, and Whittier</b> fall to another regional center, because the health district
  boundary runs through the city. If you live in one of those four, your city name is not the
  answer — your address is.
</NoticeBox>

Two neighbors matter most here. **Pasadena** is a health district of its own, and Frank D.
Lanterman Regional Center covers it — so a Pasadena family may belong to either center depending
on the block. **Whittier** is likewise a health district covered by Eastern Los Angeles Regional
Center. If your street sits near one of those seams, do not guess.

The reliable checks, in order:

1. Our <a class="link" href="/tools/regional-center-finder/">Regional Center finder</a> — enter
   your address, not just your city.
2. The state's own directory: <a class="link" href="https://www.dds.ca.gov/rc/listings/">DDS Regional Center listings</a>.
3. Call SG/PRC and read them your street address before anything else. If it is not their area,
   they will tell you which center is — and that call takes five minutes, versus the six weeks
   an intake at the wrong center can cost.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="how-intake-starts">How does intake start here?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Call the main line and say plainly: <i>"I'd like to request an intake and assessment for my
  child for Regional Center eligibility."</i> Say your child's age first — <b>under 3</b> routes
  to Early Start on a faster clock, <b>3 and older</b> to standard Lanterman intake. SG/PRC also
  publishes an admissions and assessment page describing the process.
</AnswerFirst>

<a class="link" href="https://www.sgprc.org/individuals-and-families/admissions-assessment/">SG/PRC — Admissions &amp; Assessment →</a>

SG/PRC operates from a headquarters in **Pomona**. If you are on the Altadena or Sierra Madre end
of the territory, that is a real drive across the valley — ask on the first call whether the
intake interview can be done by phone or video, and whether any meetings happen closer to you.

What to have ready. Not a perfect folder — what exists:

- Pediatrician notes, referrals, developmental screening results
- Any evaluation: speech, occupational therapy, psychological, hearing
- Preschool or school records, an IEP or IFSP if there is one
- Your own written description of a **hard day** — specific, unflattering, honest

A diagnosis helps but is not required to start. The center funds its own assessments at no cost
to you.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="the-clocks">What clocks are running?</h2>

<DeadlineCallout>
  <b>15 working days</b> from your request to initial intake <Cite>WIC §4642</Cite>.
  <b>120 days</b> from intake to a completed assessment — <b>60 days</b> where delay would risk
  your child's health, safety, or development <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite>. Under 3: <b>45 days</b>
  from referral for the Early Start evaluation and assessment <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Then
  <b>60 days</b> from the completed assessment to the written plan <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>.
</DeadlineCallout>

These are identical at all 21 centers. What is local is who picks up, where you drive, and which
providers have openings nearby. Your child's rights do not shrink because a boundary line put you
in one center rather than another.

Eligibility at **3 and older** means a developmental disability that began before 18 and amounts
to a "substantial disability" — significant functional limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas**
<Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. Under **age 5**, a child can also be found **provisionally eligible** on
limits in **at least 2** of those areas <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>. Ask about that by name if your
preschooler is told they are close but not quite.

<EmpathyCallout label="On the seven-center map">
  Learning that your county has seven regional centers, and that you might be on the wrong side
  of a health district line you have never heard of, is a genuinely absurd thing to discover at
  11pm. It is not your failure to understand. It is an administrative map from another era, and
  the fix is one phone call with your address in front of you.
</EmpathyCallout>

<h2 class="qa-h" id="if-they-say-no">What if SG/PRC says no?</h2>

A denial — of eligibility, or of a specific service — has to arrive as a written **Notice of
Action**. An informal no on the phone is not a decision and starts no clock. Ask for it in
writing, every time. From the written notice you have **60 days** to request a hearing
<Cite>WIC §4710.5</Cite>; check the dates printed on your own notice.

Free advocates exist. The **Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy (OCRA)**, part of **Disability
Rights California**, handles regional center appeals for families statewide.
