<AnswerFirst>
  San Gabriel/Pomona Regional Center serves families in <b>Los Angeles County</b> (one of seven regional centers that divide Los Angeles County by neighborhood — your address decides which serves you) — one of California's
  <b> 21 regional centers</b> providing services under the Lanterman Act
  <Cite>WIC §4500 et seq.</Cite>, regardless of your income. Once intake begins, the center
  owes you any needed assessment within <b>120 days</b> of intake <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite> —
  or, under 3, a completed Early Start evaluation and initial family service plan within
  <b>45 days</b> of the referral <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Write down the date of your
  first contact. The clock starts then.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="intake">How does intake work?</h2>

The intake process runs the same at every regional center. A formal diagnosis helps but is **not required
to start** — eligibility turns on "substantial disability," meaning significant functional
limits in **3 or more of 7 life areas** <Cite>WIC §4512</Cite>.

1. **Contact intake and say your child's age.** Under 3 routes to Early Start and the faster
   45-day clock; 3 and over goes through standard Lanterman intake.
2. **Gather what you have** — pediatrician notes, evaluations, IEP or preschool records.
   Missing pieces should not delay your request.
3. **Do the intake interview.** Describe a hard day, not your child's best day.
4. **Note the deadline.** The assessment is due within 120 days of intake <Cite>WIC §4643</Cite> —
   under 3, Early Start completes everything within 45 days of the referral
   <Cite>17 CCR §52086</Cite>. Calendar it the day you first call.
5. **Get the decision in writing.** If eligible, planning moves to your child's IPP
   <Cite>WIC §4646</Cite>; if denied, you must receive a written **Notice of Action** with
   your appeal rights.

<h2 class="qa-h" id="la-area">Which part of Los Angeles County does this center serve?</h2>

<AnswerFirst>
  Seven regional centers divide Los Angeles County between them, so "I live in LA County" is
  not enough to tell you which one is yours — <b>your address decides it</b>, and the
  boundaries follow LA County health districts, not city lines.
</AnswerFirst>

Serves one county — Los Angeles — but only the eastern San Gabriel Valley and Pomona Valley
portion of it. SGPRC is one of seven regional centers dividing LA County, and its catchment
is defined by three DDS/LA County health districts: El Monte, Foothill, and Pomona. Within
those districts it covers about 30 cities and communities: Altadena, Arcadia, Azusa, Baldwin
Park, Charter Oak, City of Industry, Claremont, Covina, Diamond Bar, Duarte, El Monte,
Glendora, Hacienda Heights, Irwindale, La Puente, La Verne, Monrovia, Pasadena (portions
only), Pomona (portions only), Rowland Heights, San Dimas, Sierra Madre, South El Monte,
Temple City (portions only), Valinda, Walnut, West Covina, and Whittier (portions only). Its
office is at 75 Rancho Camino Drive, Pomona, CA 91766. Because several cities are split at
the health-district / ZIP boundary rather than the city line, a family in Pasadena, Pomona,
Temple City, or Whittier cannot rule SGPRC in or out by city name alone and should confirm
by street address or ZIP.

### Who this center does not serve

Does NOT cover the rest of Los Angeles County outside the El Monte, Foothill, and Pomona
health districts. Specifically, neighbouring catchments belong to other centers: the
Alhambra, East Los Angeles, Northeast, and Whittier health districts — including Alhambra,
East LA, and most of Whittier — are Eastern Los Angeles Regional Center (ELARC), whose
Whittier district office serves that area; and the Central, Glendale, Hollywood-Wilshire,
and Pasadena health districts — including the City of Pasadena proper and Glendale — are
Frank D. Lanterman Regional Center. This is the sharpest ruling-out edge: SGPRC lists
Altadena and part of Pasadena among its communities (Foothill health district), while the
Pasadena health district itself is Lanterman's, so Pasadena addresses split between the two
centers. Whittier likewise appears in SGPRC's community list only in part, with the Whittier
health district assigned to ELARC. Central, South, and West LA, the San Fernando Valley, the
Antelope Valley, the South Bay/Harbor area, and Long Beach are all served by other LA County
regional centers, not SGPRC. (Caveat: no snippet gave an explicit ZIP-level exclusion list
from SGPRC's own site — the exclusions above are inferred from the published health-district
assignments of the adjacent centers, which are stated in the sources.)

If your address sits near one of these boundaries, confirm your center before you call —
being sent back to the start by the wrong center costs weeks.
