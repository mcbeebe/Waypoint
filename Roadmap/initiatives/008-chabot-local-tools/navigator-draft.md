## PAGE 1 — FIND YOURSELF ON THE MAP

### The Chabot Family Navigator
**A friendly map for families of kids with ADHD, autism, and other brains that
work differently**

*Written by a Chabot parent who's been exactly where you are. The short
version: you're not alone, it's always okay to ask, and there's a map. This
is it.*

#### How this guide works

School support can feel like a maze full of acronyms. It isn't, really. It's
**three starting points, a few magic words, and a couple of timers.** Find
your starting point below, follow the steps, and flip to page 2 whenever you
hit a word that makes your eyes cross — there's a decoder for that.

#### 🧭 STOP 1 — "Something's going on with my kid, but nobody's named it yet."

Maybe it's big feelings after school. Trouble sitting still, or making
friends, or notes coming home. You don't need a diagnosis — or anyone's
permission — to start.

1. **Talk to the teacher.** Ask the simplest question there is: *"What are
   you seeing?"* Teachers see 25 kids a day; their answer is gold.
2. **Ask for an SST.** That's just a meeting — you, the teacher, and a few
   school folks around a table comparing notes and making a simple plan.
   No forms to fear, nothing signed away. Ask the front office or your
   teacher to set one up.
3. **Or go straight to free testing.** The school will test how your child
   learns — for free — to figure out what help fits. You just have to ask
   **in writing** (the magic words are on page 2).

**The one secret nobody tells you:** the moment you ask *in writing*, a real
timer starts ticking for the school — by law. A hallway chat is friendly, but
it starts no timer. Email does both: friendly *and* ticking. (Timers, also
page 2.)

#### 🧭 STOP 2 — "A doctor gave us a diagnosis, but nothing changed at school."

Here's the plain truth: a doctor's note doesn't automatically change
anything at school. School support comes from one of **two menus**, and it's
completely okay to ask which one fits your kid:

- **A 504 plan = the small-changes list.** Sitting near the teacher.
  Movement breaks. Extra time on tests. Little adjustments, written down, so
  they happen every day in every class — not just when someone remembers.
- **An IEP = the big plan.** Actual extra teaching and services — small-group
  help, speech, occupational therapy — with written goals the school checks
  every year. More help, more meetings, more protection.

**Not sure which?** Nobody expects you to be. Ask for the free testing
(Stop 1, step 3). The results tell the team which menu fits. You lose
nothing by asking, and the testing itself often explains your kid better
than years of guessing.

#### 🧭 STOP 3 — "We already have an IEP or 504, but…"

…it's not being followed, or it's not working, or something new came up.

1. **Start with a friendly ask.** *"Could we look at how the reading help is
   going? Here's what we're seeing at home."* Most problems end right here,
   with one conversation.
2. **You can call a meeting any time.** Not just at the once-a-year review.
   Put it in writing, and the team has **30 days** to sit down with you.
3. **Before any meeting:** ask for the reports and goals ahead of time, and
   bring one page of notes about what you see at home. You are the world's
   leading expert on your kid — that page is evidence.

## PAGE 2 — THE DECODER RING, THE TIMERS & THE MAGIC WORDS

#### 🔤 The decoder ring

Every field has its alphabet soup. Here's yours, translated:

| The letters | Stand for | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| **IEP** | Individualized Education Program | The big plan: extra teaching and services, with goals, in writing. The school must deliver what's in it. |
| **504** | Section 504 (of a 1973 civil-rights law) | The small-changes list: seating, breaks, extra time — written down so they happen every day. |
| **SST** | Student Success Team | A no-pressure meeting where you and the school compare notes and try ideas. Often the first step. |
| **IDEA** | Individuals with Disabilities Education Act | The national law behind all of this: every kid gets what they need to learn, for free. |
| **Evaluation** | (also "assessment") | Free testing by school specialists to figure out how your child learns and what helps. |
| **RSP** | Resource Specialist Program | The teacher whose whole job is giving kids extra help. Chabot has one — meet them. |
| **OT** | Occupational Therapy | Help with hands and bodies: handwriting, scissors, staying comfortable and calm in a busy classroom. |
| **ESY** | Extended School Year | Summer school for kids who lose skills over long breaks — part of some IEPs. Ask in spring. |

#### ⏱️ The timers (this is the part to remember)

When you ask **in writing**, California law starts real clocks:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **DAY 0** — You send the magic words asking for testing. | **BY DAY 15** — The school hands you a testing plan to sign. | **BY DAY 60** — Testing is done and the team meets you to go over it. |

And any time you already have a plan: a written request for a team meeting
means the meeting happens **within 30 days.**

*The fine print: Cal. Ed. Code §§ 56321, 56344, 56343.5. School breaks
longer than 5 days pause the clocks — so asks sent before winter break beat
asks sent after.*

#### ✉️ The magic words (copy, paste, add your kid's name, send)

Email these to the principal. One sentence is genuinely enough — and the
moment you hit send, the timer starts.

| You want | Send this |
|---|---|
| Free testing | *"I'm requesting a special education assessment for my child, [name], under IDEA. Please send me an assessment plan."* |
| A team meeting (IEP) | *"I'd like to request an IEP team meeting for [name] to talk about [topic]. What dates work?"* |
| The small-changes list (504) | *"I'd like to talk about a 504 plan for [name]. Who should I meet with, and when?"* |
| Your kid's file | *"I'm requesting a complete copy of [name]'s student records, including all assessments."* |

**Two tiny habits that change everything:** keep a copy of every email, and
ask nicely every time. Friendly-and-in-writing is the whole game — you can
always firm up later if an ask goes unanswered, but you rarely have to.

#### ⭐ Three promises from parents who've been there

1. **Ask in writing, every time.** Not because anyone's the enemy — because
   writing starts timers, makes memories official, and keeps everyone's
   promises easy to keep.
2. **Lead with friendly.** The teachers and specialists want your kid to
   succeed. Start every ask like you believe that — because it's true, and
   because this team is yours for years.
3. **Never go alone if you don't want to.** Bring a friend, a Group parent,
   or a free DREDF-trained advocate to any meeting. Someone on your side of
   the table changes the air in the room.
