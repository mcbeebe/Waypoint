## PAGE 1 — FIND YOUR PATH

### The Chabot Family Navigator
**A friendly map for families of kids with ADHD, autism, and other brains
that work differently**

*Written by a Chabot parent who's been exactly where you are. The short
version: you're not alone, it's always okay to ask, and you and the school
are on the same team. Here's the map.*

#### How this guide works

School support can feel like a maze full of acronyms. It isn't, really.
It's **three paths, a few magic words, and a shared schedule.** Find the
path that sounds like your family, follow the steps, and flip to page 2
whenever you hit a word that makes your eyes cross — there's a decoder
for that.

#### 🌱 PATH 1 — "Something's going on with my kid, but nobody's named it yet."

Maybe it's big feelings after school. Trouble sitting still, or making
friends, or notes coming home. You don't need a diagnosis to start — just
curiosity, and a team. The school wants to figure this out with you.

1. **Start with the teacher.** Ask the simplest question there is: *"What
   are you seeing?"* Teachers see 25 kids a day; their answer is gold —
   and they'll be glad you asked.
2. **Ask for an SST.** That's just a meeting — you, the teacher, and a few
   school folks around a table comparing notes and trying ideas together.
   No forms to fear, nothing signed away. The front office can set one up.
3. **Or ask for free testing.** The school can test how your child learns —
   for free — so everyone understands what helps. Just ask **in writing**
   (the magic words are on page 2).

**The friendly secret:** when you ask in writing, an official schedule
begins — set by California law so that nothing gets lost in a busy school
year. A hallway chat is lovely; an email is a hallway chat the calendar
remembers. Do both!

#### 💬 PATH 2 — "A doctor gave us a diagnosis, but school looks the same."

Here's the plain truth, with no villain in it: doctors and schools run on
different paperwork, so a diagnosis doesn't automatically flow into the
classroom. Bridging that gap is a conversation — and there are **two menus**
to talk through together:

- **A 504 plan = the small-changes list.** Sitting near the teacher.
  Movement breaks. Extra time on tests. Little adjustments, written down,
  so every adult at school knows them — even a substitute on day one.
- **An IEP = the big plan.** Actual extra teaching and services —
  small-group help, speech, occupational therapy — with written goals the
  whole team celebrates and updates every year.

**Not sure which fits?** Nobody expects you to be. Ask for the free testing
(Path 1, step 3). The results help you *and* the school pick together —
and testing often explains your kid better than years of guessing.

#### 🌟 PATH 3 — "We have an IEP or 504, and something needs a tune-up."

Plans are living things. Kids grow, classrooms change, and what worked in
fall might need adjusting by spring. That's normal — and it's what the
team is for.

1. **Start with a friendly check-in.** *"Could we look at how the reading
   help is going? Here's what we're seeing at home."* Most tune-ups happen
   right here, in one conversation.
2. **You can gather the team any time.** Not just at the once-a-year
   review. Ask in writing, and the meeting lands on the calendar within
   **30 days**.
3. **Before any meeting:** ask for the reports and goals ahead of time, and
   bring one page of notes about what you see at home. You know your kid
   best — that page helps the team see the whole child.

## PAGE 2 — THE DECODER RING, THE SCHEDULE & THE MAGIC WORDS

#### 🔤 The decoder ring

Every field has its alphabet soup. Here's yours, translated:

| The letters | Stand for | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| **IEP** | Individualized Education Program | The big plan: extra teaching and services, with goals, in writing, that the whole team works toward together. |
| **504** | Section 504 (a 1973 civil-rights law) | The small-changes list: seating, breaks, extra time — written down so every adult knows, every day. |
| **SST** | Student Success Team | A no-pressure meeting where you and the school compare notes and try ideas. Often the first step. |
| **IDEA** | Individuals with Disabilities Education Act | The national promise behind all of this: every kid gets what they need to learn, for free. |
| **Evaluation** | (also "assessment") | Free testing by school specialists so everyone understands how your child learns and what helps. |
| **RSP** | Resource Specialist Program | The teacher whose whole job is giving kids extra help. Chabot has one — go say hi. |
| **OT** | Occupational Therapy | Help with hands and bodies: handwriting, scissors, staying comfortable and calm in a busy classroom. |
| **ESY** | Extended School Year | Summer learning for kids who lose skills over long breaks — part of some IEPs. Ask in spring. |

#### 📅 The shared schedule (this is the part to remember)

When you ask **in writing**, California sets a schedule everyone can count
on — so you always know what happens next:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **DAY 0** — You send the magic words asking for testing. | **BY DAY 15** — The school sends you a testing plan to sign. | **BY DAY 60** — Testing is done, and the team meets to talk it through with you. |

And any time you already have a plan: a written request for a team meeting
puts it on the calendar **within 30 days.**

*The fine print: Cal. Ed. Code §§ 56321, 56344, 56343.5. School breaks
longer than 5 days pause the schedule — so asks sent before winter break
land sooner than asks sent after.*

#### ✉️ The magic words (copy, paste, add your kid's name, send)

Email these to the principal. One sentence is genuinely enough — it gives
the school exactly what it needs to get started.

| You'd like | Send this |
|---|---|
| Free testing | *"I'm requesting a special education assessment for my child, [name], under IDEA. Please send me an assessment plan."* |
| A team meeting (IEP) | *"I'd like to request an IEP team meeting for [name] to talk about [topic]. What dates work for the team?"* |
| The small-changes list (504) | *"I'd like to talk about a 504 plan for [name]. Who should I meet with, and when?"* |
| Your kid's file | *"I'm requesting a complete copy of [name]'s student records, including all assessments. Thank you!"* |

**Two tiny habits that change everything:** keep a copy of every email, and
lead with kindness every time. Writing keeps everyone organized; kindness
keeps everyone on the same team. That combination solves almost everything.

  you make it. (SB 946)

#### ⭐ Three promises from parents who've been there

1. **Ask in writing, every time.** Writing keeps busy people organized,
   makes plans official, and keeps everyone's promises easy to keep —
   yours included.
2. **Lead with friendly.** The teachers and specialists want your kid to
   succeed. Start every ask like you believe that — because it's true, and
   because this team is yours for years.
3. **Bring a buddy whenever you like.** A friend, a Group parent, or a
   free DREDF-trained advocate is welcome at any meeting. A second set of
   ears makes everything easier.
