The Escalation Ladder
Regional Center · climb one rung at a time — each rung creates the paper trail the next rung needs. Every rung has a one-tap letter, and the tone matches the rung: collaborative first, firmer only when a rung goes unanswered.
1
Ask, in writing + request an IPP review
30 DAYS
TONE · FRIENDLY & COLLABORATIVE
Name the gap warmly and ask for help closing it — your coordinator is your partner until proven otherwise. Putting it in writing isn't hostile; it just starts the record. The RC must hold the review within 30 days of your written request — no webinar, orientation, or "process" gates this right.

W&I Code §4646.5(b)
2
Request the written NOA — appeal if refused
60 DAYS
TONE · FIRM BUT PROFESSIONAL
Any denial, reduction, or termination must come as a written Notice of Action. No NOA yet? Request one — politely, citing the requirement. Appeal within 60 days; file within 30 days and existing services continue during the appeal ("aid paid pending").

W&I Code §4710.5 — windows verified at build
3
§4731 complaint to the RC director
20 WORK DAYS
TONE · FORMAL
For rights violations (not just service disagreements): the director must respond with a written proposed resolution within 20 working days. Escalates to DDS if unresolved.
✍️ File the §4731 complaint →
W&I Code §4731 · template already in Letters
4
Bring in a free advocate
FREE
TONE · YOU + AN ADVOCATE
OCRA (Office of Clients' Rights Advocacy) has an attorney assigned to every Regional Center — free, by law. Disability Rights California takes systemic cases. You never have to climb alone, and you never have to pay.
📞 Find your OCRA advocate →
Why a ladder, not a list. Order matters twice over. The paper trail: rung 1's written record is rung 2's evidence; rung 2's NOA is what an advocate at rung 4 works with. And the relationship: your coordinator is a years-long partner — starting collaborative keeps the door open, and makes the record look reasonable if you ever do have to climb.
