HOME REDESIGN · OUTPUT OF THE 20-PERSONA AUDIT · AUG 29, 2026
Three concepts. One question: what is Home's job?
Twenty exhausted-caregiver personas audited every pixel of the current Home page: 10 confirmed failures (dead $0 snapshot, blocking English tutorial, canned empathy, duplicate "WAYPOINT NOTICED" cards, no-undo dismissals, jargon pills, false profile nudges, silent snoozes — plus two already fixed: the inert RC phone and the Early Start clock) and an emergent critique: 15 scrollable sections, 7 competing CTAs, and no single job. Five redesign philosophies competed; a 3-judge panel kept the three strongest and most distinct. Every concept below resolves all ten findings — they differ in what Home fundamentally is.
A · UNANIMOUS №1
"the app decides"
The Next Right Thing
A deterministic triage engine renders exactly one card — one honest kicker with provenance, one concrete action (calls are tel: links, replies are read-first), one "why this is first," one deferral with a stated return time. Below: one tell-Waypoint input and one collapsed everything-else ledger (Tools kept, one tap down). Calm state is earned and true: "Nothing has a clock on it today. You can close the app."
Wins: the 45-second 3am parent · contradictions structurally impossible · TTS-linear · highest feasibility (existing derivations feed the ladder).
Costs: veterans lose glance density · push loop becomes load-bearing · looks "small" in demos.
B · TOP-3, ALL JUDGES
"the app talks"
Front Desk
Home is the first two turns of a conversation. Waypoint speaks one true, dated, child-named sentence (or one honest question — never a confident guess). The parent answers by chip, voice, or typing in their language. Cards exist only as artifacts a conversation produced and the parent pinned. Openers and chips compose locally — only free text touches the API. The Tools shelf stays for parents who won't talk to an AI.
Wins: trilingual by construction · lowest literacy barrier · crisis entry always one tap · Home and Ask AI become one thread.
Costs: discovery slows to dialogue pace · veterans must build their own board · free-text latency on rural LTE · forces finishing es/vi app-wide.
C · TOP-3, ALL JUDGES
"the app shows the true state"
Caseboard
An instrument board of dated facts: one fixed row per live obligation (NEW REPLY → PAST DUE → DUE TODAY → WAITING → STALLED), each with child, source tag, and text state label. A sensor line is the provenance contract. Everything derived is quarantined in a Findings drawer — question-framed, inputs disclosed, correctable. Zero pep, zero pitch, zero demo data.
Wins: the burned veteran operator (the audit's unanimous power-user ask) · trust as the product · fact/hypothesis split kills confident-wrong claims.
Costs: sparse for newcomers · discovery one tap down · most backend work (event log, synced ledgers).

RECOMMENDATION
Build A — The Next Right Thing — with two organs grafted in
Take Caseboard's sensor line (the provenance contract that makes the calm state believable) and Front Desk's tell-Waypoint composer (crisis vocabulary + Ask-AI fallthrough). Ship in 3 phases: ① pure triage engine + one card + calm state (the ten findings die here) · ② everything-else ledger with undo + contacts · ③ tell-Waypoint row + the push loop that makes "we'll tell you" true. Caseboard stays on the shelf as a veteran mode — a second renderer over the same engine, not a rework.
Decision needed: A, B, C, or A+organs. Nothing is built until you pick.
