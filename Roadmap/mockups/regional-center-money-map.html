Regional Center Money Map

The exact business process: how a family becomes a Regional Center consumer, how services get approved and paid on the traditional path and the Self-Determination path, and precisely where Waypoint — and a camp, a respite agency, an ABA provider — plug in. Every clock and rule below was verified against statute or DDS directives (Aug 2026); the few unverifiable internals are flagged.

The cast

FAMILY / CONSUMER — the child is the RC "consumer" SERVICE COORDINATOR — the family's RC case manager (W&I §4647) RC APPROVAL CHAIN — supervisor → clinical/director review → exec. director for exceptions VENDORED PROVIDERS — camps, respite, ABA agencies with an RC vendor number FMS — the family's fiscal agent in self-directed models WAYPOINT — free app + independent facilitator DDS — sets the rules and rates statewide

STAGE 0 · BECOMING A CONSUMER — EVERY FAMILY STARTS HERE

1 · Family contacts the Regional Center (intake)Family → RC

Under 3: Early Start (the RCs also run IDEA Part C). Age 3+: Lanterman Act application. Waypoint free app: tells the family they likely qualify and drafts the referral

↓
2 · Assessment & eligibility decision≤120 days from intake (≤60 if delay is risky) · W&I §4643

RC clinicians assess; eligibility = substantial disability from a qualifying condition (autism, ID, CP, epilepsy, or "5th category") beginning before 18. Denials carry appeal rights. Waypoint: assessment prep, records checklist, appeal letters

↓
3 · Service coordinator assigned → IPP writtenIPP within 60 days of assessment completion · W&I §4646GATE: nothing is purchasable unless it's in the IPP

The IPP (Individual Program Plan) is the master document: goals + the services the RC will provide. It's negotiated in a meeting between family and service coordinator, and it is the gate — every dollar on either path below traces to it. A review meeting must be held within 30 days whenever the family requests one (§4646.5(b)) — the family's main forcing lever.

Two money rules that shape everything: (1) Payer of last resort — the RC must use "generic resources" first (school district, Medi-Cal, private insurance; W&I §4646.4, §4659), which is why RCs often demand an insurance denial before buying a service. (2) Free to the family — all family cost-participation programs are fully repealed (family fees ended July 2024; the Parental Fee Program July 2025). Nothing on this page costs the family out-of-pocket.

PATH A · TRADITIONAL PURCHASE OF SERVICE — HOW MOST VENDORS GET PAID

A1 · Service coordinator drafts a POS authorizationSC → RC approval chain

For each IPP service: a Purchase of Service authorization naming a vendored provider, service code, units, dates, rate. The SC does not approve alone: supervisor/manager sign-off is standard, clinical or director second-level review applies to high-cost or exceptional requests, and POS-policy exceptions need the executive director. Initial authorizations often run only 3–6 months. Exact dollar thresholds are internal and vary by RC — not published.

↓
A2 · Vendored provider delivers, bills monthly via eBillingRC pays ~2–6 weeks after each service month

Vendorization (Title 17) is a one-time gate per provider — but it never obligates any RC to purchase. Billing over the authorized units is unrecoverable, which is why authorization tracking is the core control in any provider's software.

The camp example, Path A: since AB 136 (2021) restored camps, social recreation, and nonmedical therapies, a camp can be paid three ways: as a vendored provider on a POS authorization; unvendored via service code 459 ("participant-directed" — the family picks a general-population camp and a traditional-system FMS pays it); or by family reimbursement. So even in the traditional system, not everything requires vendorization anymore.
Where Waypoint fits on Path A today: nowhere as a paid provider. This is the audit's code-108 finding — there is no confirmed service code for coaching the parents of a child consumer, which is exactly DDS question #0. Until that's answered, Waypoint's Path-A role is the free app helping families win IPP battles.

PATH B · SELF-DETERMINATION PROGRAM — WHERE WAYPOINT GETS PAID

SDP is an alternative to Path A: instead of the RC buying services one authorization at a time, the RC converts the consumer's services into an annual individual budget the family directs. Same state money, different decision-maker.

B1 · OrientationSCDD (sole statewide provider since 1 Apr 2026)

Mandatory, free: two 2-hour sessions. The family decides to proceed. Waypoint: finds the family (free app), explains SDP, gets them scheduled

↓
B2 · Individual budget developed & certifiedRC planning team certifiesNo statutory deadline — the 30-day IPP-meeting rule is the only lever

Under AB 143 (2025): the initial budget = the most recent 12 months of authorized services (not spend), plus amounts for IPP-identified unmet needs. This is why child budgets are often modest — a child with little authorization history starts low, and the "unmet needs" add-on is where facilitation earns its keep.

↓
B3 · Person-centered plan writtenRC reimburses up to $1,000 · code 024WAYPOINT REVENUE №1 — no vendorization needed

The family picks anyone with PCP training — vendored or not — to write the plan; the RC pays as a purchase reimbursement after the written plan is delivered (case-by-case increases for language access/complexity). Paid per family, per authorization by the service coordinator.

↓
B4 · Transition supports deliveredRC pays hourly, up to 40 hrs · code 099 · via eBillingWAYPOINT REVENUE №2 — requires one 099 vendorization

Hands-on help getting enrolled: budget worksheets, plan drafting, FMS selection. Delivered by a 099-vendored provider on a per-family authorization; more hours possible case-by-case. This is the one vendorization Waypoint needs in Phase 1 — a week-1 task.

↓
B5 · Spending plan built → RC certifies itGATE: RC certification (AB 143)

Family + facilitator translate the certified budget into lines: services, providers, rates — summing exactly to the budget. The RC certifies completeness and IPP linkage (a "cost-effectiveness" test is coming; the DDS directive defining it is late), and budgets growing ≥$20k year-over-year get reported to DDS. Waypoint may never appear as a service provider on a plan it facilitates (W&I §4685.8) — the facilitation line itself is the one legal exception, priced by the family.

↓
B6 · Family picks an FMSFMS fee paid by the RC on top of the budget ($230–925/mo by model + headcount)

Three models: 315 Fiscal Agent (pays vendors/businesses), 316 Co-Employer (FMS is employer of record for the family's workers), 317 Fiscal/Employer Agent (family is the direct employer; FMS runs payroll/taxes). The fee never draws down the family's budget.

↓
B7 · IPP amended → enrolledSC + family sign

SDP participation is written into the IPP; the budget year starts.

↓
B8 · Ongoing: providers invoice the FMS; the FMS pays from the budgetBudget dollars, disbursed by the FMSWAYPOINT REVENUE №3 — the facilitation line, at the family's agreed price

Here's the SDP flexibility: providers on the spending plan need no vendorization — a camp, a tutor, a swim coach, a parent-chosen aide simply invoice the FMS. What they do need: FMS-run background checks for direct-care work, EVV for personal-care services, and HCBS-compliant settings. The independent facilitator is paid the same way — from the budget (which is not increased to cover it: every facilitation dollar trades off against service dollars, which is why the price is the family's call).

Annual renewalBudget + plan renewed ≤60 days after the budget year ends; FMS notified ≥14 days before

The one place SDP has hard clocks — and the recurring-revenue moment for facilitation.

Reality check on speed: orientation → enrolled runs ~3–12 months in practice (~8 months average); there is no enforceable deadline on initial budget certification or plan approval. Bottlenecks: orientation scheduling, budget negotiation, FMS onboarding, RC staff capacity. Compressing this pipeline is Waypoint's facilitation product — and why "hours per family" and "days per stage" are on the owner scorecard.
