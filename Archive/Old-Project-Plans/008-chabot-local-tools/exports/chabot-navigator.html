The Chabot Family Navigator

A friendly map for families of kids with ADHD, autism, and other brains that work differently.

Written by a Chabot parent who's been exactly where you are. The short version: you're not alone, it's always okay to ask, and you and the school are on the same team. Here's the map.

Find your path

School support can feel like a maze full of acronyms. It isn't, really. It's three paths, a few magic words, and a shared schedule. Find the path that sounds like your family — and whenever a word makes your eyes cross, the decoder below has you covered.

🌱 Path 1 — "Something's going on with my kid, but nobody's named it yet."

Maybe it's big feelings after school. Trouble sitting still, or making friends, or notes coming home. You don't need a diagnosis to start — just curiosity, and a team. The school wants to figure this out with you.

  1. Start with the teacher. Ask the simplest question there is: "What are you seeing?" Teachers see 25 kids a day; their answer is gold — and they'll be glad you asked.
  2. Ask for an SST. That's just a meeting — you, the teacher, and a few school folks around a table comparing notes and trying ideas together. No forms to fear, nothing signed away. The front office can set one up.
  3. Or ask for free testing. The school can test how your child learns — for free — so everyone understands what helps. Just ask in writing (the magic words are below).
The friendly secret: when you ask in writing, an official schedule begins — set by California law so that nothing gets lost in a busy school year. A hallway chat is lovely; an email is a hallway chat the calendar remembers. Do both!

💬 Path 2 — "A doctor gave us a diagnosis, but school looks the same."

Here's the plain truth, with no villain in it: doctors and schools run on different paperwork, so a diagnosis doesn't automatically flow into the classroom. Bridging that gap is a conversation — and there are two menus to talk through together:

  1. A 504 plan = the small-changes list. Sitting near the teacher. Movement breaks. Extra time on tests. Little adjustments, written down, so every adult at school knows them — even a substitute on day one.
  2. An IEP = the big plan. Actual extra teaching and services — small-group help, speech, occupational therapy — with written goals the whole team celebrates and updates every year.
Not sure which fits? Nobody expects you to be. Ask for the free testing (Path 1, step 3). The results help you and the school pick together — and testing often explains your kid better than years of guessing.

🌟 Path 3 — "We have an IEP or 504, and something needs a tune-up."

Plans are living things. Kids grow, classrooms change, and what worked in fall might need adjusting by spring. That's normal — and it's what the team is for.

  1. Start with a friendly check-in. "Could we look at how the reading help is going? Here's what we're seeing at home." Most tune-ups happen right here, in one conversation.
  2. You can gather the team any time. Not just at the once-a-year review. Ask in writing, and the meeting lands on the calendar within 30 days.
  3. Before any meeting: ask for the reports and goals ahead of time, and bring one page of notes about what you see at home. You know your kid best — that page helps the team see the whole child.

📅 The shared schedule

This is the part to remember. When you ask in writing, California sets a schedule everyone can count on — so you always know what happens next:

Day 0

You send the magic words asking for testing.

By day 15

The school sends you a testing plan to sign.

By day 60

Testing is done — the team meets to talk it through with you.

And any time you already have a plan: a written request for a team meeting puts it on the calendar within 30 days.

The fine print: Cal. Ed. Code §§ 56321, 56344, 56343.5. School breaks longer than 5 days pause the schedule — so asks sent before winter break land sooner than asks sent after.

✉️ The magic words

Copy, add your kid's name, and email the principal. One sentence is genuinely enough — it gives the school exactly what it needs to get started.

You'd like free testingI'm requesting a special education assessment for my child, [name], under IDEA. Please send me an assessment plan.
You'd like a team meeting (IEP)I'd like to request an IEP team meeting for [name] to talk about [topic]. What dates work for the team?
You'd like the small-changes list (504)I'd like to talk about a 504 plan for [name]. Who should I meet with, and when?
You'd like your kid's fileI'm requesting a complete copy of [name]'s student records, including all assessments. Thank you!
Two tiny habits that change everything: keep a copy of every email, and lead with kindness every time. Writing keeps everyone organized; kindness keeps everyone on the same team. That combination solves almost everything.

⭐ Three promises from parents who've been there

✦Ask in writing, every time. Writing keeps busy people organized, makes plans official, and keeps everyone's promises easy to keep — yours included.
✦Lead with friendly. The teachers and specialists want your kid to succeed. Start every ask like you believe that — because it's true, and because this team is yours for years.
✦Bring a buddy whenever you like. A friend, a Group parent, or a free DREDF-trained advocate is welcome at any meeting. A second set of ears makes everything easier.
